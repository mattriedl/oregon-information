Christmas in Oregon 2026: Best Towns, Lights, Trains & Holiday Events

2026 dates, prices and booking tips for Oregon's biggest holiday markets, light displays, boat parades and Santa trains, plus the towns worth a winter weekend.

📅 November 9, 2026⏱ 12 min read
Home › Blog › Christmas in Oregon 2026: Best Towns, Lights, Trains & Events

Planning Christmas in Oregon? The season runs from the week before Thanksgiving to New Year's Day. The biggest draws are the Silverton Christmas Market, Portland's Grotto Festival of Lights and ZooLights, the Christmas Ships on the Columbia and Willamette rivers, and steam-train rides with Santa from Hood River to the coast. This guide gives the 2026 dates, prices and booking tips for each, plus the Oregon Christmas towns worth a weekend away.

Quick answer: Christmas in Oregon 2026 at a glance

  • Biggest single event: the Silverton Christmas Market at the Oregon Garden Resort, Nov. 20 to Dec. 31, with more than 2 million lights.
  • Best Christmas lights in Portland: The Grotto (Nov. 27 to Dec. 30) for a quiet walk with choirs, ZooLights (Nov. 20 to Jan. 3) for families, and Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway (Nov. 27 to Dec. 31) to drive through.
  • Free and classic: the Christmas Ships Parade, about 80 lighted boats on 15 December nights, in its 72nd season.
  • Christmas trains: The Polar Express from Hood River, the Holiday Express in Portland, the Candy Cane Express on the north coast and Sumpter Valley's Christmas trains in Eastern Oregon.
  • Book early: popular Silverton nights sell out, Shore Acres needs a parking reservation, and holiday trains sell out weeks ahead.

What Are the Best Christmas Towns in Oregon?

The best Christmas towns in Oregon are Silverton, Sunriver, Ashland and Jacksonville, with Hood River close behind thanks to its Polar Express train. Each one turns a holiday tradition into a reason to stay the night: a German-style market in Silverton, a six-week resort celebration in Sunriver, a downtown light festival in Ashland and Victorian house tours in Jacksonville.

Silverton: Oregon's Christmas market town

Silverton, about 15 miles east of Salem in the Willamette Valley, is home to the state's biggest Christmas market. It has its own section below. While you're in town, the historic downtown along Main Street and Water Street is easy to walk, and the surrounding hills are dotted with Christmas tree farms.

Sunriver: a snowy Central Oregon getaway

Sunriver, a resort community south of Bend in Deschutes County, is the place to go if you want a good chance of snow. According to Visit Central Oregon, the Village at Sunriver kicks off the season in late November with its Grand Illumination, a day-long event with Santa's arrival, live music, a tree lighting and fireworks. Sunriver Resort's Traditions program runs from late November through New Year's, with a nightly light show, gingerbread house displays, U-cut Christmas trees, sleigh rides, and guided snowshoe and snowmobile tours. The Village also has ice skating, with rental skates for kids and adults.

Ashland: the Festival of Light

Ashland opens its holiday season the day after Thanksgiving with the Festival of Light, organized by the Ashland Chamber of Commerce and Travel Ashland. Last year's 33rd festival started at 3 p.m. on the Plaza with music and local vendors. At 5 p.m., Santa's parade wound to the Plaza, and Santa flipped the switch on more than 1 million lights from Lithia Way to the Plaza rooftops, as Ashland.news reported. The Friday after Thanksgiving falls on Nov. 27 in 2026. Confirm the schedule with Travel Ashland before you go.

Jacksonville: a Victorian Christmas

Fifteen minutes from Ashland, Jacksonville is a gold-rush town whose entire downtown is a National Historic Landmark district. On Nov. 28 and 29, 2026, the Historic Jacksonville group hosts Victorian Christmas at the 1870s Beekman House Museum (470 E. California St.). Costumed docents lead one-hour tours on the origins of Christmas traditions, starting every 20 minutes from noon to 3 p.m. Tickets are $10 for adults and $5 for children 12 and under (Historic Jacksonville calendar). Pair it with a stroll down California Street and dinner in Ashland.

Silverton Christmas Market: What to Know

The Silverton Christmas Market runs Nov. 20 to Dec. 31, 2026, at the Oregon Garden Resort, and it's open most dates in that window. According to Travel Oregon, the event has more than 2 million lights. It also has an authentic German vendor market selling imported ornaments and nutcrackers, glühwein (mulled wine), 28 local vendors, snowless tubing, live music, fire pits and visits with Santa through Dec. 23. New for 2026 is a full-size ice skating rink inside a winter tent.

Buy tickets ahead of time. Ticket prices change by date, and popular nights, especially December weekends, often sell out. If you'd rather not drive home in the dark and rain, the Oregon Garden Resort sells hotel-and-ticket packages that include market admission, breakfast, parking and entry to The Oregon Garden. Silverton is also a good base for Silver Falls State Park, whose waterfalls run high in winter.

Where Are the Best Christmas Lights in Oregon?

The best Christmas lights in Oregon are The Grotto and ZooLights in Portland, the drive-through Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway, and the Holiday Lights at Shore Acres State Park on the south coast. Here's how they differ.

The Grotto's Christmas Festival of Lights (Portland)

The Grotto, a Catholic sanctuary in Northeast Portland, is the calm option: a quarter-mile walk through lighted gardens, with choirs singing in the Chapel of Mary, puppet shows at the top of each hour and the Grotto Carolers at the bottom of each hour. Most visits take 60 to 90 minutes. The 2026 festival runs Nov. 27 to Dec. 30 (closed Christmas Day), from 5 to 9:30 p.m. Online tickets are $17.95 for ages 12 and up and $8.95 for ages 3 to 11, with cheaper "value nights" early in the week. Parking is free. On busy nights, use the free shuttle from the Parkrose/Sumner Park & Ride on the MAX Red Line (The Grotto).

ZooLights at the Oregon Zoo (Portland)

ZooLights is the big family pick. It's a walk-through route of about 1.25 miles, plus a zoo train past dozens of life-size animal lanterns. The 2026 season runs Nov. 20, 2026 to Jan. 3, 2027, opening nightly at 4:30 p.m. The zoo is closed Dec. 25 and a few November nights. Tickets are priced by date, from $13 to $35 for adults, and new this year is about 10,000 square feet of heated, covered space at the Wildlife Pavilion. Most animals aren't on view in the evening (Oregon Zoo).

Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway

On a rainy night, drive the lights instead. Winter Wonderland covers more than two miles of the racetrack at 1940 N Victory Blvd with over a million lights. It's priced per carload rather than per person, and runs Nov. 27 to Dec. 31, 2026, except a few special-event nights around the start of December (Winter Wonderland). Check the event site for that year's excluded nights before you go.

Holiday Lights at Shore Acres State Park (Coos Bay)

For Christmas on the Oregon coast, the volunteer-run Holiday Lights at Shore Acres State Park near Coos Bay fill the park's formal garden with hundreds of thousands of colored lights. The Friends of Shore Acres have run it for more than 30 years. In 2025 the display ran Nov. 27 to Dec. 31, and every visitor needed a timed parking reservation: $10 per vehicle, with arrival slots from 4:30 to 7:30 p.m. Reservations are released on a rolling seven-day window (Oregon Parks and Recreation Department). Watch the state parks newsroom for 2026 details, and come early to watch winter surf from the cliffs (see our Oregon coast storm watching guide).

Sea of Lights at the Oregon Coast Aquarium (Newport)

In Newport, the Oregon Coast Aquarium's Sea of Lights opens on select Friday and Saturday evenings from late November until just before Christmas. In 2025 it ran from 5 to 8 p.m. on eight nights between Nov. 28 and Dec. 20, according to Discover Newport. Newport also holds a lighted boat parade on Yaquina Bay alongside its bayfront tree lighting in early December.

When Is the Christmas Ships Parade in Portland?

The Christmas Ships Parade sails on 15 nights in December, and it's free to watch from shore. The tradition began in 1954, and 2026 is its 72nd season. About 80 volunteer-owned boats, from 14 to 65 feet long, take part over the season on the Columbia and Willamette rivers around Portland and Vancouver. On select nights the fleet forms a lighted procession nearly two miles long (Christmas Ships).

Each night follows a different route, so check the nightly route maps on the official parade schedule and pick a public riverfront park. The organizers say shore is the best place to see the whole fleet lit up. Arrival times shift with river conditions and bridge lifts, and weather cancellations are decided close to start time. In 2026 the boats also tie up for visits at the St. Helens public dock on Dec. 12 and at Portland's RiverPlace Marina on Dec. 19 and 20. Riverfront restaurants with views take reservations, and some book up months in advance.

Are There Christmas Trains in Oregon?

Yes. Oregon has at least five holiday trains in 2026, from The Polar Express in Hood River to steam-powered Santa trains in Portland, on the north coast and in the Blue Mountains. Most of them sell out, so book as soon as you know your dates.

The Polar Express (Mt. Hood Railroad, Hood River)

The Mt. Hood Railroad's Polar Express runs Nov. 20 to Jan. 2 from the depot in downtown Hood River. The ride is about 90 minutes round trip. Elves serve hot cocoa and a cookie, and Santa boards on the return trip to meet every child and hand out a bell. Standard seats range from $59 to $79 for adults and $49 to $69 for kids 2 to 12, depending on the date. Tickets went on sale Sept. 1, so weekend trains go fast (Mt. Hood Railroad).

Holiday Express (Oregon Rail Heritage Center, Portland)

The Oregon Rail Heritage Foundation's Holiday Express pulls a historic steam locomotive along the Willamette River from the Oregon Rail Heritage Center at 2250 SE Water Ave. Santa and his elves ride along. It runs Nov. 27 to Dec. 27, with tickets at $35 for adults, $25 for children 3 to 13, $32 for seniors and military, and $105 for a family pass. Choose an early-dusk departure to see the lights (Oregon Rail Heritage Foundation).

Candy Cane Express and the Rockaway Beach tree-lighting train (north coast)

The Oregon Coast Scenic Railroad runs its steam-powered Candy Cane Express from Nov. 27 to Dec. 23. The one-hour ride includes hot chocolate, cookies, carols and a visit with Santa, from $23. On Nov. 27 it also runs a special train from Garibaldi to the tree-lighting ceremony in Rockaway Beach, from $26 (Oregon Coast Scenic Railroad).

Sumpter Valley Railroad Christmas trains (Eastern Oregon)

For snow and small-town charm, the Sumpter Valley Railroad near Sumpter and Baker City runs Christmas trains from its McEwen depot on Dec. 11, 12 and 13, 2026. The City of Sumpter holds its traditional Christmas parade at 5 p.m. on Dec. 11. Pets aren't allowed on the Christmas trains (Sumpter Valley Railroad).

Does It Snow in Oregon at Christmas?

A white Christmas is rare in Portland, the Willamette Valley and on the coast, but likely in the Cascades and common east of the mountains. West of the Cascades, December is usually cool and wet, with highs in the 40s. Snow in Bend, Sunriver, Baker City or Joseph is far more likely, and the ski areas on Mt. Hood and Mt. Bachelor are usually open by Christmas in a normal snow year. Our Oregon weather by season guide explains why the state splits so sharply at the Cascades.

If you're crossing a mountain pass, check ODOT's TripCheck for road cameras and chain requirements before you leave. Carry chains or use traction tires in winter. Conditions on Santiam, Willamette and Government Camp passes can change within an hour.

How to Plan a Christmas Trip in Oregon

  • Book in this order: trains first (they sell out soonest), then Silverton on a weekend night, then Shore Acres parking when your seven-day reservation window opens.
  • Use value nights: The Grotto and ZooLights both charge less early in the week. Monday through Thursday evenings are also less crowded.
  • Dress for rain, not snow, in the valley and on the coast: a waterproof jacket and boots matter more than a heavy coat. East of the Cascades, pack for below-freezing nights.
  • Combine a region into one weekend: Portland (Grotto, ZooLights, Christmas Ships); the mid-valley (Silverton plus Salem); Southern Oregon (Ashland's Festival of Light plus Jacksonville); or the coast (Shore Acres plus storm watching).
  • Shop without sales tax: Oregon has no sales tax, so holiday markets and downtown shops cost what the tag says.

Christmas in Oregon: Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best Christmas towns in Oregon?

Silverton (for the Silverton Christmas Market), Sunriver (for snow and a six-week resort celebration), Ashland (for the Festival of Light the day after Thanksgiving) and Jacksonville (for Victorian Christmas tours of the Beekman House) are the standouts. Hood River adds The Polar Express train.

When is the Silverton Christmas Market in 2026?

The Silverton Christmas Market runs Nov. 20 to Dec. 31, 2026, on most dates, at the Oregon Garden Resort in Silverton. Prices vary by date, children 5 and under get in free, and popular nights often sell out, so buy tickets ahead.

Where are the best Christmas lights in Portland, Oregon?

The Grotto's Christmas Festival of Lights (Nov. 27 to Dec. 30) and ZooLights at the Oregon Zoo (Nov. 20 to Jan. 3) are the most popular walk-through displays. Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway is a drive-through show of over a million lights, priced per car.

Is the Christmas Ships Parade free?

Yes. The Christmas Ships Parade is free to watch from public riverfront parks along the Columbia and Willamette rivers. It sails on 15 nights in December, and the nightly route maps are posted on the official Christmas Ships parade schedule.

Are there Christmas trains in Oregon?

Yes. In 2026 you can ride The Polar Express from Hood River, the Holiday Express steam train in Portland, the Candy Cane Express on the Oregon Coast Scenic Railroad and the Sumpter Valley Railroad's Christmas trains in Eastern Oregon. Most sell out, so book early.

Do you need reservations for the Shore Acres holiday lights?

Yes. In 2025 every vehicle needed a timed parking reservation, at $10 per car, booked through Oregon State Parks on a rolling seven-day window. Watch the Oregon Parks and Recreation Department newsroom for the 2026 booking details.
