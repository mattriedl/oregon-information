Christmas Lights in Portland, Oregon: Peacock Lane, Drive-Thrus & Free Displays (2026)

Where to see Portland's best holiday lights in 2026, from free Peacock Lane and neighborhood streets to The Grotto, ZooLights and the drive-through at Portland International Raceway.

📅 November 10, 2026⏱ 13 min read
Home › Blog › Christmas Lights in Portland, Oregon 2026: Best Displays & Dates

Looking for the best Portland Christmas lights this year? Start with Peacock Lane, the free "Christmas Street" in Southeast Portland (Dec. 15 to 31, 2026), then pick one ticketed show: The Grotto for a quiet walk with choirs, ZooLights for families, or Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway if you'd rather stay dry in the car. This guide gives 2026 dates, hours and prices for each, plus the free neighborhood streets locals drive, the Christmas Ships schedule and the best light displays an hour or less from downtown.

Quick answer: Portland Christmas lights 2026

  • Best free display: Peacock Lane, Dec. 15 to 31 from 6 p.m., with pedestrian-only nights on Dec. 15 and 16.
  • Best walk-through: The Grotto (Nov. 27 to Dec. 30, $17.95 online) for calm and choirs; ZooLights (Nov. 20 to Jan. 3, $13 to $35) for kids.
  • Best drive-through: Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway, Nov. 27 to Dec. 31, from $38 per car online.
  • Best free boat show: the Christmas Ships Parade, 15 nights from Dec. 3 to 20.
  • Beat the crowds: go Monday to Thursday, arrive at opening, and use value nights at The Grotto and ZooLights.

Where Are the Best Christmas Lights in Portland?

The best Christmas lights in Portland are Peacock Lane, The Grotto's Christmas Festival of Lights, ZooLights at the Oregon Zoo and Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway. Pick by budget, weather and who's coming:

  • Free and walkable: Peacock Lane, plus dozens of decorated neighborhood streets across the metro area.
  • Families with young kids: ZooLights, with its train ride and animal lanterns.
  • Quiet and traditional: The Grotto, a Catholic sanctuary with choirs in its chapel.
  • Rainy nights, toddlers or grandparents: Winter Wonderland, which you see from your own car.

If you only have one night in Portland, pair Peacock Lane (free) with a ticketed show the same evening. For the rest of Oregon's holiday season, including Shore Acres on the coast and the state's Santa trains, see our Christmas in Oregon guide.

Peacock Lane: Dates, Hours and Tips

Peacock Lane is lit nightly from Dec. 15 to 31, 2026, starting at 6 p.m., and it's free. The display covers SE Peacock Lane between SE Stark and SE Belmont streets in Southeast Portland, and according to the Peacock Lane residents' website, every house on it has decorated for Christmas since 1932.

In 2026 the pedestrian-only nights are Dec. 15 and 16, when no cars are allowed on the lane. On other nights you can drive through slowly, but walking is the better experience: cars crawl bumper to bumper, and Portland Police may close the lane to vehicles at any time for safety. The residents' site lists the lights running until 10 p.m. in one place and 11 p.m. in another, so plan to arrive well before 10.

A free cocoa booth opens at 6 p.m. from Dec. 15 to 24, while supplies last, and donations are welcome. Peacock Lane is non-commercial: no selling, flyers, petitioning or asking visitors for donations. Park a few blocks away on a side street, respect driveways, and keep the sidewalk moving.

The Grotto and ZooLights: Portland's Walk-Through Light Shows

The Grotto and ZooLights are Portland's two big ticketed walk-through displays, and both open by Thanksgiving weekend. The Grotto is calmer and cheaper; ZooLights is bigger and built for kids.

The Grotto's Christmas Festival of Lights

The Grotto, a Catholic sanctuary in Northeast Portland, turns its gardens into a quarter-mile walk of lights from Nov. 27 to Dec. 30, 2026 (closed Christmas Day), 5 to 9:30 p.m. Choirs sing in the Chapel of Mary, a puppet show starts at the top of each hour and carolers perform at the bottom of each hour. Most visits take 60 to 90 minutes.

Online tickets are $17.95 for ages 12 and up and $8.95 for ages 3 to 11 (ages 2 and under free). Gate prices are $2 higher, and the gate doesn't take cash. Value nights, Monday to Thursday from Nov. 30 to Dec. 3 and Dec. 7 to 10, drop online tickets to $15.95 and $6.95. Parking is free in the main lot and on nearby streets; on busy nights, a free shuttle runs from the Parkrose/Sumner Park & Ride on the MAX Red Line (The Grotto).

The Grotto Christmas Festival of Lights📍 8840 NE Skidmore St, Portland, OR 97220 (entrance at NE 85th Ave and Sandy Blvd)📅 Nov. 27 to Dec. 30, 2026, 5 to 9:30 p.m.; closed Dec. 25🎫 $17.95 adult, $8.95 youth online; value nights $15.95 / $6.95🌐 thegrotto.org

ZooLights at the Oregon Zoo

ZooLights runs Nov. 20, 2026 to Jan. 3, 2027, opening nightly at 4:30 p.m., with closing times that vary from 8:30 to 10:30 p.m. It's closed Nov. 24 to 26 and Dec. 25. The route is a little over a mile (about 1.25 miles), and the zoo train runs past dozens of life-size animal lanterns. New for 2026 is 10,000 square feet of heated, covered space at the Wildlife Pavilion.

Tickets are priced by night: $13 to $35 for adults and $13 to $30 for youth, with the lowest prices on value nights (Nov. 20 to 23, Nov. 30 to Dec. 3 and Jan. 3). Members get 20% off regular-priced tickets. Jan. 1 and 2 are 21-and-over nights. Your ZooLights ticket doubles as a TriMet fare on your visit date, valid on buses, MAX and the Portland Streetcar, and MAX trains stop at Washington Park station next to the entrance. Zoo parking is limited and paid, and there's free park-and-ride parking at Sunset Transit Center (Oregon Zoo).

ZooLights📍 Oregon Zoo, 4001 SW Canyon Rd, Portland, OR 97221📅 Nov. 20, 2026 to Jan. 3, 2027, from 4:30 p.m.; closed Nov. 24-26 and Dec. 25🎫 $13 to $35 adult, $13 to $30 youth, by date🌐 oregonzoo.org/lights

Is There a Drive-Through Christmas Light Show in Portland?

Yes. Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway is Portland's big drive-through light show, with more than a million lights along over two miles of racetrack. It runs Nov. 27 to Dec. 31, 2026, and all proceeds support the Sunshine Division's hunger-relief work.

Hours are 5 to 10 p.m. Monday to Thursday and 4:30 to 11 p.m. Friday to Sunday, and 4:30 to 11 p.m. every night from Dec. 16 to 25. Tickets are priced per vehicle, not per person. For a standard car, SUV or minivan, online prices are $38 Monday to Thursday, $45 on Sundays, $49 on Fridays and Saturdays and $50 on peak nights from Dec. 16 to 25; gate prices are $4 to $5 more. A VIP Fast Lane on peak nights costs $85. Limos, RVs and passenger vans pay $85 online. Discount $35 tickets, good on any drive-through night, go on sale at Safeway and Albertsons customer service desks the week of Thanksgiving while supplies last (Winter Wonderland pricing).

Cars aren't allowed on the special event nights at the start of the season: Bike the Lights on Nov. 29 and the Lights & Leashes dog walk on Nov. 30 and Dec. 1, when you pay $16 per adult and $5 per child online to see the show on foot or by bike. Dec. 2 and 9 are sensory-friendly nights with reduced flashing. The event's pages list slightly different date ranges for the car-free nights, so confirm on the Winter Wonderland site before you go. Chains and studded tires aren't allowed on the track.

Winter Wonderland📍 Portland International Raceway, 1940 N Victory Blvd, Portland, OR 97217📅 Nov. 27 to Dec. 31, 2026 (no cars on special event nights)🎫 $38 to $50 per car online; $35 discount tickets at Safeway and Albertsons🌐 winterwonderlandportland.com

Which Portland Neighborhoods Have the Best House Lights?

Beyond Peacock Lane, the best free house lights are on streets where whole blocks decorate together, such as the Starkwood area in East Portland, Walnut Lane in Tigard and Ridgewood Drive in Gladstone. These are private homes: drive slowly, stay off lawns and keep noise down. Details below come from coverage of past seasons.

Starkwood (East Portland)

In the Hazelwood neighborhood, homes in the Starkwood subdivision along SE 111th Avenue north of SE Stark Street, including the cul-de-sacs on SE Ash Street, SE Ankeny Street and SE Pine Court, have decorated together for decades. The East Portland News counted 38 participating homes, lit from 5 to 11 p.m.

Walnut Lane (Tigard)

SW Walnut Lane in Tigard is lit nightly from about Thanksgiving through Dec. 31, from sunset to 9:30 p.m. Sunday to Thursday and an hour later on Fridays and Saturdays, according to PDX Parent's holiday lights list.

Ridgewood Drive and Lark Avenue (Gladstone and Milwaukie)

In Gladstone, Ridgewood Drive, a dead-end street with three cul-de-sacs, and nearby Clayton Way decorate heavily. A few minutes away in Milwaukie, the animated "Schilling Lights on Lark" display on SE Lark Avenue has about 55,000 lights. BikePortland strings these together into a seven-mile holiday-lights bike loop from downtown Gladstone using the Trolley Trail.

Beaverton and Hillsboro

On the west side, Beaverton has run free Winter Lights displays around City Park and City Hall in December. Hillsboro's free drive-through Lightopia ended its original format after the 2025 season, and the city said it would spread holiday lights around town instead, OPB reported. Check the city's website for what's planned in 2026.

When Do the Christmas Ships Sail in 2026?

The Christmas Ships Parade sails on 15 nights from Dec. 3 to Dec. 20, 2026, and it's free to watch from shore. The volunteer-run parade has sailed since 1954, and about 80 boats, 14 to 65 feet long, take part over the season. Most parades run 7 to 9 p.m., with separate Columbia and Willamette fleets on many nights (Christmas Ships).

  • Willamette Fleet nights: Dec. 7, 8, 9, 11, 15, 16, 17 and 18, starting from RiverPlace Marina and cruising toward Johns Landing, the Fremont Bridge or Milwaukie.
  • Columbia Fleet nights: the same dates, starting from the M. James Gleason Boat Ramp and cruising past North Portland Harbor, Vancouver or the I-205 bridge.
  • Combined fleet nights: Dec. 3, 4 and 5 on the Columbia, Dec. 12 at St. Helens, Dec. 13 on the Multnomah Channel near Scappoose, and the season finales on Dec. 19 (to Lake Oswego) and Dec. 20 (to the St. Johns Bridge).

Good free viewing spots on the Willamette are the RiverPlace Esplanade, Tom McCall Waterfront Park and the South Waterfront Greenway. On the Columbia, try NE Marine Drive between NE 43rd Avenue and Lemon Island, or the Gleason Boat Ramp (4325 NE Marine Dr; $5 parking). Each night's route and timing is on the official parade schedule, and weather can cancel a night close to start time.

Downtown Portland: Pioneer Courthouse Square's Christmas Tree

Downtown's big holiday light is the tree in Pioneer Courthouse Square, usually a 75-foot Douglas fir, lit the day after Thanksgiving. A Christmas tree has gone up in the square every year since it opened in 1984. The 2025 ceremony, the 41st, ran from 5:30 to 6:45 p.m. on the Friday after Thanksgiving, with a sing-along led by Thomas Lauderdale of Pink Martini before Santa switched on nearly 10,000 lights (Travel Portland). The Friday after Thanksgiving falls on Nov. 27 in 2026; check The Square's event calendar for this year's time.

The square is at 701 SW 6th Ave, in the middle of downtown's shopping district, and Oregon has no sales tax.

Christmas Lights Near Portland: Silverton and Keizer

The best Christmas lights within an hour of Portland are at the Silverton Christmas Market at The Oregon Garden and in Keizer, just north of Salem.

Silverton Christmas Market (The Oregon Garden)

About an hour south of Portland, the Silverton Christmas Market surrounds the Oregon Garden Resort's main lodge with 2 million lights, plus a German vendor market, ice skating, snowless tubing and live music. It runs Nov. 20 to Dec. 31, 2026, on most dates. On some nights when the market is closed, including Dec. 24 and 25, the resort sells "Lights Only" hotel packages to see the lights without the crowds (Oregon Garden Resort). Buy market tickets ahead; December weekends sell out.

Keizer: Miracle of Christmas and GLOW

Keizer's Miracle of Christmas is a free neighborhood display in the Gubser neighborhood, on a marked route of about 3.5 miles that draws close to 20,000 vehicles a season. The city's guidance: take I-5 exit 260 (260B southbound), expect long lines on weekend nights, bring canned or non-perishable food to donate, and if you want to walk, park at Whiteaker Middle School or Gubser Elementary. There are no public restrooms (City of Keizer). The city's most recently posted schedule ran from early December to Dec. 26, nightly from 6 to 10 p.m.; check the city's news page for 2026 dates.

Keizer also hosted the ticketed GLOW Christmas Light Festival at Roto-Rooter Park, the minor league ballpark, for the first time in 2025, open from Black Friday through the end of the year. 2025 prices were $19.99 for ages 13 and up and $14.99 for kids 4 to 12, plus $5 parking (Salem Reporter). Check the organizer's site for 2026 dates. Make a night of it with dinner in Salem.

Tips for Seeing Portland Christmas Lights

  • Go early in the week and early in the evening. Weeknights are cheaper at all three ticketed shows, lines are shortest at opening, and the cheapest dated tickets sell out first.
  • Dress for rain, not snow. December in Portland is usually cool and wet, with highs in the 40s. Wear a waterproof jacket and shoes; see our Oregon weather by season guide.
  • Save Peacock Lane for a weeknight or the pedestrian-only nights (Dec. 15 and 16), and visit before Christmas Eve if you want free cocoa.
  • Check conditions on icy nights. Freezing rain occasionally hits the Portland area in December; check ODOT's TripCheck and the organizer's social media for closures before you head out.

Christmas Lights in Portland: Frequently Asked Questions

When is Peacock Lane open in 2026?

Peacock Lane is lit nightly from Dec. 15 to 31, 2026, starting at 6 p.m. It's free. The pedestrian-only nights, when no cars are allowed, are Dec. 15 and 16. Arrive before 10 p.m. to be safe.

Can you drive through Peacock Lane?

Yes, except on the pedestrian-only nights (Dec. 15 and 16 in 2026). Traffic is very slow, and police can close the lane to cars at any time for safety, so most people park nearby and walk.

What is the best drive-through Christmas light show in Portland?

Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway, with more than a million lights over two-plus miles of racetrack. It runs Nov. 27 to Dec. 31, 2026, and costs $38 to $50 per car online, depending on the night.

Are there free Christmas lights in Portland?

Yes. Peacock Lane, the Pioneer Courthouse Square tree, the Christmas Ships Parade and neighborhood streets like the Starkwood area in East Portland, Walnut Lane in Tigard and Ridgewood Drive in Gladstone are all free.

How much are ZooLights tickets in 2026?

ZooLights tickets are priced by date: $13 to $35 for adults and $13 to $30 for youth. Value nights (Nov. 20 to 23, Nov. 30 to Dec. 3 and Jan. 3) are the cheapest, and Oregon Zoo members get 20% off regular-priced tickets.

When do the Christmas Ships sail in 2026?

The Christmas Ships Parade sails on 15 nights from Dec. 3 to Dec. 20, 2026, on the Willamette and Columbia rivers. Most parades run from about 7 to 9 p.m., and it's free to watch from riverfront parks.
