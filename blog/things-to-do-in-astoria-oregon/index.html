Things to Do in Astoria, Oregon: The Complete Visitor Guide

Every major Astoria attraction with current hours and prices, what's free, what's open in winter, Goonies etiquette and a one- or two-day itinerary.

📅 November 21, 2026⏱ 13 min read
Home › Blog › Things to Do in Astoria, Oregon: Top Attractions, Prices & Tips

Astoria sits where the Columbia River meets the Pacific, and it packs more history, river views and quirky landmarks into a small hillside town than almost anywhere in Oregon. The best things to do in Astoria Oregon are climbing the Astoria Column, spending a morning at the Columbia River Maritime Museum, walking the Riverwalk to see ships and sea lions, and driving 10 miles out to the Peter Iredale shipwreck at Fort Stevens State Park. This guide covers every major attraction with current 2026 hours and prices, what is free, what is open in winter and how to plan one or two days.

Quick answer: top things to do in Astoria

  • Must-do: climb the 164 steps of the Astoria Column (free to climb; parking is $10 per car and good for a year).
  • Best museum: the Columbia River Maritime Museum, open daily 9:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.; adults $18.
  • Best free activity: the Astoria Riverwalk, with the Astoria-Megler Bridge, cargo ships and barking sea lions at the East Mooring Basin.
  • Movie fans: the Oregon Film Museum in the old county jail (adults $6) and a respectful look at the Goonies house from the street.
  • Nearby: the Peter Iredale shipwreck at Fort Stevens State Park and Fort Clatsop, the Lewis and Clark winter camp.
  • 2026 bonus: the Astoria Column turned 100 this year (dedicated July 22, 1926).

What Are the Top Attractions in Astoria, Oregon?

The top attractions in Astoria, Oregon are the Astoria Column, the Columbia River Maritime Museum, the Riverwalk, the Flavel House and Oregon Film Museum, and the Peter Iredale shipwreck at nearby Fort Stevens State Park. Founded in 1811 as a fur-trading post, Astoria is the oldest American settlement west of the Rockies, and its Victorian houses stack up a steep hillside above a working river port. It is the seat of Clatsop County and the northern starting point of US 101 on the Oregon Coast.

Astoria is about 95 miles from Portland via US 30, or roughly two hours by car. Most attractions are within a few minutes of downtown. Plan on one full day for the town itself and a second day for Fort Stevens, Fort Clatsop and the beaches at Seaside and Cannon Beach.

Astoria Column: Hours, Parking and the Climb

The Astoria Column is a 125-foot tower on Coxcomb Hill with a 164-step spiral staircase to a viewing deck; the park is open daily from 5 a.m. to 10 p.m., climbing is free and parking costs $10 per vehicle, good for one year. On a clear day the deck looks over the mouth of the Columbia, the Astoria-Megler Bridge, Youngs Bay, Saddle Mountain and, on the best days, all the way to the Pacific.

The column was dedicated on July 22, 1926, so 2026 marks its centennial. Its outside is wrapped in a spiral mural by Italian artist Attilio Pusterla that tells the region's history, from the Clatsop people and Captain Robert Gray's 1792 entry into the river to the arrival of the railroad. The Clatsop County Historical Society's Heritage Museum is running a temporary exhibit, "A View from the Top: Celebrating 100 Years of the Astoria Column."

A long-running tradition is buying a small balsa-wood glider in the gift shop and launching it from the top. If you'd rather skip the narrow stairs, the hilltop plaza still has big views. The surrounding Astor Park has picnic tables and forest trails, including the Cathedral Tree Trail, which climbs through the woods from the neighborhoods below.

Best Museums in Astoria

Astoria's best museums are the Columbia River Maritime Museum, the Flavel House, the Oregon Film Museum and the Heritage Museum, and all four are open daily. The maritime museum is the big one; the other three are run by the Clatsop County Historical Society and are small enough to see in under an hour each.

Columbia River Maritime Museum

The mouth of the Columbia is one of the most dangerous river bars in the world, and this riverfront museum tells that story well: shipwrecks, Coast Guard rescues, bar pilots, fishing and the river's role in trade. Admission includes the lightship Columbia, a floating lighthouse moored outside (open 11 a.m. to 3 p.m.; call for day-of availability). The museum has been expanding, and its new Warnock Building and Mariners Hall were set to open to the public on November 20, 2026. Parking on site is free.

Columbia River Maritime Museum📍 1792 Marine Dr, Astoria, OR 97103📅 Daily 9:30 a.m.-5 p.m.; closed Thanksgiving and Christmas Day💰 Adults $18, seniors 65+ $15, ages 6-17 $8, 5 and under free; active-duty military free📞 503-325-2323🌐 crmm.org

Flavel House Museum

This 1880s Queen Anne mansion was built for Captain George Flavel, one of the first Columbia River bar pilots, and it is furnished in period style. The tower, wraparound porches and gardens are worth seeing even from outside. In December the historical society hosts its annual Holiday Tea and Plum Pudding (December 5, 2026, 1 to 4 p.m.).

Oregon Film Museum

Housed in the old Clatsop County Jail, the building that appears in the opening jailbreak scene of The Goonies (1985), this small, hands-on museum covers movies filmed in Oregon. Plan on 30 to 45 minutes.

Heritage Museum and Uppertown Firefighters Museum

The Heritage Museum, in Astoria's former city hall, covers Native history, immigrant communities, logging and canneries. The free Uppertown Firefighters Museum on Marine Drive shows antique fire engines but is closed for the season as of late 2026.

Hanthorn Cannery Museum

Out at Pier 39 on the east side of town, this free museum fills part of a historic cannery building with photos and artifacts from the salmon-canning era that built Astoria. The pier is private commercial property with limited parking.

Astoria Riverwalk, Trolley and Sea Lions

The Astoria Riverwalk is a free, mostly flat paved and boardwalk path that follows the Columbia River for several miles, from the Port of Astoria in the west to the lagoons near Tongue Point in the east. You pass under the Astoria-Megler Bridge, watch bar pilots and huge cargo ships, and stroll past old cannery piers, breweries and the Maritime Memorial.

The Astoria-Megler Bridge is about 4 miles long and opened in 1966; it is the longest continuous truss bridge in North America. Pedestrians aren't allowed except during the annual Great Columbia Crossing run and walk, usually held in October.

The Astoria Riverfront Trolley, a restored 1913 streetcar nicknamed "Old 300," runs along the Riverwalk tracks with volunteer conductors narrating local history; fares have been $1 per ride. Its 2026 season ran from March 20 to November 1 (1 to 4 p.m. Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday, and 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekends, not in rain), so look for it again next spring at old300.org.

At the East Mooring Basin near 36th Street, California sea lions haul out on the docks. Travel Oregon lists it as the main sea lion haul-out on the Oregon Coast. The males arrive in late summer and stay through fall, winter and early spring, so you'll usually hear them before you see them. Nearby, Buoy Beer Company's taproom has a glass floor panel where you can sometimes watch sea lions below.

Goonies Filming Locations in Astoria

Astoria is where much of The Goonies was filmed, and fans can see the old jail (now the Oregon Film Museum), the Flavel House and the outside of the Goonies house. The Goonies house on 38th Street is a private home. After years of crowds the previous owner closed it in 2015 and later reopened foot traffic with a request that visitors walk up the lane rather than drive, and the house was sold in 2023 to new owners who are fans of the film.

Rules can change, so follow posted signs, park legally on the main streets, keep noise down and never go onto the property uninvited. Movie fans can also drive to Ecola State Park and Haystack Rock in Cannon Beach for the film's beach scenes.

Fort Stevens, Fort Clatsop and Day Trips Near Astoria

The two essential trips near Astoria are Fort Stevens State Park, about 10 miles west, and Fort Clatsop in Lewis and Clark National Historical Park, about 5 miles southwest. Both are easy half-day outings and pair well together.

Fort Stevens State Park and the Peter Iredale shipwreck

The rusting bow of the Peter Iredale, a four-masted sailing ship that ran aground on Clatsop Spit in 1906, sits right on the beach and is the most photographed shipwreck in Oregon. The 4,300-acre park, near the town of Hammond in Warrenton, also has the old military fort and gun batteries that guarded the river mouth from the Civil War through World War II (a Japanese submarine fired on the area in June 1942), plus lakes, bike paths and the South Jetty viewing platform. Its campground has more sites than any other Oregon state park.

Fort Stevens requires a day-use parking permit: $10 per day for Oregon residents or $12 for non-residents, or a 12-month permit ($60 residents, $75 non-residents), per Oregon State Parks. Visit the wreck at low tide for the best photos and keep an eye on the surf.

Fort Stevens State Park📍 Hammond area of Warrenton, OR 97121📅 Open year-round💰 Day-use parking $10/day Oregon residents, $12/day non-residents (campers with a hangtag are exempt)🌐 stateparks.oregon.gov

Fort Clatsop (Lewis and Clark National Historical Park)

Lewis and Clark's Corps of Discovery spent the wet winter of 1805-06 here. A replica of their fort sits in the forest beside a visitor center with exhibits and a film, and the Netul River Trail and the 6.5-mile Fort to Sea Trail start nearby. Rangers in period clothing often give demonstrations in summer.

Fort Clatsop Visitor Center📍 92343 Fort Clatsop Rd, Astoria, OR 97103📅 9 a.m.-5 p.m. in the cool season, 9 a.m.-6 p.m. in summer; closed Thanksgiving, Dec. 24 and Dec. 25💰 $10 per adult (ages 16+), valid 7 days; 15 and under free; card only; America the Beautiful passes accepted🌐 nps.gov/lewi

Beach towns and the Washington side

Seaside's Promenade is about 17 miles south and Cannon Beach about 25 miles south on US 101, or stay closer in quiet Gearhart. For a longer drive, start our Oregon coast road trip itinerary in Astoria and head south.

Free Things to Do in Astoria

The best free things to do in Astoria are walking the Riverwalk, watching the sea lions at the East Mooring Basin, climbing the Astoria Column (parking costs $10) and touring the Hanthorn Cannery Museum. A few more ideas that cost nothing:

  • Victorian house walk: wander the hillside streets between downtown and the Column, where restored 19th-century homes line nearly every block.
  • Maritime Memorial: a riverside park under the bridge with names of people lost at sea and a great view of ships passing.
  • Astoria Sunday Market: from Mother's Day into the fall, up to 150 vendors fill four downtown blocks with food, produce and crafts. The 2026 season opened May 10.
  • Window-shop downtown: Commercial and Duane streets have bookstores, antique shops and galleries.

Things to Do in Astoria in Winter

Astoria is a good winter destination because almost every major attraction stays open year-round, crowds thin out and hotel rates usually drop. Expect rain and wind from November through March; the coast gets most of its rain in these months (see Oregon weather by season). Good winter plans:

  • Rainy-day museums: the Maritime Museum and the three historical society museums are all open daily.
  • Storm and ship watching: the South Jetty at Fort Stevens and the riverfront hotels are front-row seats for big winter swells; read our Oregon coast storm watching guide before you go.
  • Sea lions: their numbers at the East Mooring Basin are highest from fall through early spring.
  • December events: the Holiday Tea and Plum Pudding (Dec. 5, 2026) and the free Wildlife Center of the North Coast Winter Festival (Dec. 5, 2026). For a wider list, see Christmas in Oregon.
  • Whale watching: gray whales pass the north coast from mid-December into January; see our whale watching guide.

Winter storms can close or slow US 30 and US 26 with downed trees and high water, so check ODOT TripCheck before driving from Portland.

Things to Do in Astoria Oregon in One or Two Days

One day in Astoria is enough for the Column, the Maritime Museum, downtown and the Riverwalk; add a second day for Fort Stevens and Fort Clatsop.

Day one: town and river

  • Morning: start at the Astoria Column for clear early views, then drive down to the Columbia River Maritime Museum when it opens at 9:30 a.m.
  • Lunch: downtown, where seafood shacks, bakeries and brewpubs are a few blocks apart.
  • Afternoon: the Flavel House and Oregon Film Museum (they are around the corner from each other), then a look at the Goonies house from the street.
  • Evening: walk the Riverwalk east to the East Mooring Basin sea lions and watch the sunset behind the bridge.

Day two: forts and beaches

  • Morning: Fort Clatsop when it opens at 9 a.m.
  • Midday: Fort Stevens for the Peter Iredale (time it for low tide), the old batteries and the South Jetty.
  • Afternoon: continue south to Seaside or Cannon Beach, or loop back to Portland.

FAQ

Is Astoria, Oregon worth visiting?

Yes. Astoria combines a historic river port, Victorian architecture, one of Oregon's best museums, the Astoria Column and easy access to Fort Stevens, Fort Clatsop and the north coast beaches. It works well as a day trip from Portland or a two-day base.

How much does it cost to go up the Astoria Column?

Climbing the Astoria Column is free. Parking on Coxcomb Hill costs $10 per vehicle, and the pass is good for one year. The park is open daily from 5 a.m. to 10 p.m.

Can you visit the Goonies house in Astoria?

You can view the outside of the Goonies house, but it is a private home. Walk up the lane instead of driving, follow posted signs, stay off the property and keep noise down for the neighbors.

How far is Astoria from Portland?

Astoria is about 95 miles northwest of Portland, roughly a two-hour drive on US 30 along the Columbia River. You can also take US 26 to the coast and drive north on US 101 through Seaside.

What is there to do in Astoria when it rains?

Head indoors to the Columbia River Maritime Museum, the Flavel House, the Oregon Film Museum and the Heritage Museum, all open daily.

Do you need a parking pass for Fort Stevens State Park?

Yes. Fort Stevens requires an Oregon State Parks day-use parking permit: $10 per day for Oregon residents or $12 per day for non-residents, unless you are camping in the park or have a 12- or 24-month permit.
