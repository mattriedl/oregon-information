Oregon Ski Resorts: Where to Ski on Mt. Hood, Near Bend and Beyond

Which Oregon ski resort fits your trip, when each one opens for 2026-27, what lift tickets cost and the Sno-Park and chain rules to know before you drive up.

📅 November 16, 2026⏱ 13 min read
Home › Blog › Oregon Ski Resorts: Every Ski Area, 2026-27 Opening Dates & Prices

Oregon has about a dozen ski areas, from a few small local hills to Mt. Bachelor, one of the biggest resorts in the country. The best Oregon ski resorts for most people are on Mt. Hood, about 60 miles from Portland, and at Mt. Bachelor, 22 miles from Bend. Smaller, cheaper hills near Eugene, Ashland and Baker City are the locals' choice. This guide covers every ski area in Oregon, with 2026-27 opening dates, mountain stats, lift ticket prices, snow tubing and the Sno-Park and chain rules you need to know before you drive up.

Quick answer: Oregon ski resorts at a glance

  • Biggest: Mt. Bachelor near Bend (4,323 acres). It plans to open on December 4, 2026.
  • Closest to Portland: Mt. Hood Skibowl, Timberline and Summit Pass (about 60 miles), and Mt. Hood Meadows (about 67 miles).
  • Longest season: Timberline, which usually skis from mid-November to late May and runs summer skiing on the Palmer Snowfield.
  • Cheapest days: small hills like Anthony Lakes, Cooper Spur and Mt. Ashland on weekdays, where adult tickets were about $55 last season.
  • Before you drive: many ski area lots are Sno-Parks, so you need a permit ($4 a day, $25 a season) from November 1 to April 30. Carry chains or traction tires.

How Many Ski Resorts Are in Oregon?

Oregon has about a dozen ski areas, and 11 of them run chairlifts or tows today. The state trade group Ski Oregon lists 12: Anthony Lakes, Cat Ski Mt. Bailey, Cooper Spur, Hoodoo, Mt. Ashland, Mt. Bachelor, Mt. Hood Meadows, Mt. Hood Skibowl, Spout Springs, Timberline Lodge, Warner Canyon and Willamette Pass. Summit Pass in Government Camp isn't on that list; Timberline runs it as its beginner area.

Mt. Bailey near Diamond Lake is guided snowcat skiing, not a lift-served resort, and Warner Canyon is a small community hill. Spout Springs in the Blue Mountains closed after the 2017-18 season, and it had not reopened as of late 2024, so check before you plan a trip there.

They fall into five areas:

  • Mt. Hood (Clackamas and Hood River counties): Timberline, Summit Pass, Mt. Hood Meadows, Skibowl and Cooper Spur.
  • Central Oregon: Mt. Bachelor and Hoodoo.
  • Central Cascades (Highway 58): Willamette Pass.
  • Southern Oregon: Mt. Ashland, Mt. Bailey and Warner Canyon near Lakeview.
  • Eastern Oregon: Anthony Lakes.

When Does Ski Season Start in Oregon?

Oregon ski season usually starts between mid-November and mid-December and runs into April. Mt. Bachelor and Timberline often ski into late May. Opening day depends on snow. A warm November can push openings back by weeks.

Last season showed how much that matters. Mt. Bachelor got only about 140 inches of snow in 2025-26, its lowest total in 50 years, and didn't open until December 23, 2025. A normal year brings about 400 inches.

Ski Resorts on Mt. Hood (Near Portland)

Mt. Hood has five ski areas, the most of any mountain in Oregon, and all of them are an easy day trip from Portland. Timberline, Summit Pass and Skibowl sit along Highway 26 at Government Camp, about 60 miles from downtown. Mt. Hood Meadows is a few miles farther up Highway 35, and Cooper Spur is on the mountain's quieter northeast side, closer to Hood River. Highway 26 runs through Sandy on the way up.

Timberline Lodge

Timberline is the only Oregon resort with skiing in summer. It has 1,685 skiable acres and 4,540 feet of vertical from the top of the Palmer lift (8,540 feet) down to Summit Pass in Government Camp, which the resort calls the longest vertical in the U.S. The winter season usually runs from mid-November through late May, and the upper Palmer Snowfield keeps summer skiing going after that.

The 1930s lodge at 6,000 feet is a National Historic Landmark. Last season, night skiing ran on Friday and Saturday nights from Christmas through March 6 on the Bruno's and Pucci lifts. Parking is free and first come, first served, but you need a Sno-Park permit in the state-maintained lots.

Timberline Lodge ski area📍 Timberline Highway above Government Camp, off Highway 26📅 Typically mid-November to late May, plus summer on the Palmer Snowfield🎫 Last season: adults $129 off-peak, $161 peak; kids 6 and under free🌐 timberlinelodge.com

Summit Pass

Summit Pass is a small, gentle hill right beside Highway 26 in Government Camp, with a base at 4,000 feet. Timberline runs it as a learning area, and Timberline lift tickets include it. A Summit Pass-only adult ticket was $60 for a full day last season, and there's a tubing and sledding hill next door.

Mt. Hood Meadows

Meadows is the biggest ski area on Mt. Hood, with 2,150 acres, 11 chairlifts and 2,777 feet of vertical on the mountain's southeast side. It has a big mix of terrain, from wide groomers to steep chutes, plus night skiing and a Nordic center. It's about 67 miles from Portland and 35 miles from Hood River on Highway 35. All Meadows parking lots are part of the Oregon Sno-Park system, so every car needs a permit.

Mt. Hood Meadows📍 Highway 35, Mt. Hood, OR (about 67 miles east of Portland)📅 Opening date set by snow; check the website🎫 Sno-Park permit required in all lots🌐 skihood.com

Mt. Hood Skibowl

Skibowl sits right on Highway 26 in Government Camp, so it's usually the first stop for skiers driving from Portland. It has 960 acres and about 1,500 feet of vertical, and it's often described as the largest night-skiing area in the United States, with four lit chairlifts and 34 runs open after dark. Skibowl also runs a large tubing park (see snow tubing below). Its top is only about 5,000 feet, so it needs a good snow year.

Cooper Spur Mountain Resort

Cooper Spur is a tiny, friendly hill with 50 acres of beginner and intermediate terrain, one double chair and a rope tow. It's the cheapest lift-served skiing on Mt. Hood: last season's prices were $54 for adults and $41 for juniors 7-14, and kids 6 and under and seniors 70+ ski free. It's an Indy Pass partner and has a tubing center. Last season it ran on weekends plus Friday nights, so check the 2026-27 schedule before you go.

Skiing Near Bend: Mt. Bachelor and Hoodoo

For skiing near Bend, Oregon, you have two choices: Mt. Bachelor, 22 miles west of town on the Cascade Lakes Highway, and Hoodoo, on Santiam Pass about 20 miles past Sisters. Central Oregon's high, dry side of the Cascades usually gets drier snow than Mt. Hood, which is a big reason so many skiers end up moving to Bend.

Mt. Bachelor

Mt. Bachelor is the largest ski resort in Oregon (it calls itself the largest in the Pacific Northwest), with 4,323 skiable acres, 3,365 feet of vertical, 12 lifts and a 9,065-foot summit. The top of the lift system is at the summit of the volcano. The mountain averages about 400 inches of snow a year. Mt. Bachelor is on the Ikon Pass, and it does not require lift or parking reservations for day visitors or passholders.

The Nordic Center grooms 56 km of trails for classic and skate skiing, plus 10 km of snowshoe trails. Mt. Bachelor is in Deschutes County; check the Cascade Lakes Highway (Highway 372) on TripCheck before you drive up.

Mt. Bachelor📍 Cascade Lakes Highway (Highway 372), 22 miles west of Bend📅 2026-27 opening planned for December 4, 2026🎫 Online tickets from $99 (advance); window $164-$224 adult; Ikon Pass🌐 mtbachelor.com

Hoodoo Ski Area

Hoodoo is a mid-size, family-friendly mountain on Highway 20 at Santiam Pass, with 806 acres, 1,035 feet of vertical and five chairlifts. It sits about 20 miles from Sisters, 80 miles from Eugene and 95 miles from Salem, so it pulls skiers from both Central Oregon and the Willamette Valley. Last season adult full-day tickets were $79 ($89 on peak days), and kids 10 and under skied free.

Last season Hoodoo ran night skiing Wednesday through Saturday from 4 to 9 p.m. starting in late December, and the Autobahn Tubing Park has six 800-foot tube lanes with a handle tow.

Ski Resorts Near Eugene

The closest ski resort to Eugene is Willamette Pass, on Highway 58 about 20 miles past Oakridge. Hoodoo, about 80 miles away via Highway 126 and 20, is the other easy day trip.

Willamette Pass has 555 lift-served acres, 1,563 feet of vertical, five chairlifts and a conveyor, with a 6,683-foot summit. Its steepest run, RTS, reaches about 52 degrees, and the resort grooms 20 km of cross-country trails. The resort plans to open on December 11, 2026, and says it has tripled its snowmaking for 2026-27. It advertises advance lift tickets from $19, and kids 12 and under ski free with its Power Kids Pass.

Southern and Eastern Oregon Ski Areas

Outside the Cascades' big names, Oregon's smaller ski areas are cheap, uncrowded and run partly by nonprofits.

Mt. Ashland

Mt. Ashland, above Ashland near the California border, is a nonprofit (the Mt. Ashland Association) and has run as a community ski area since 1964. The summit is 7,500 feet, and the area has six lifts: three triple chairs, two double chairs and the First Act conveyor. Its signature terrain is the Bowl, a steep glacial cirque. Lights on select Thursday, Friday and Saturday nights allow twilight skiing until 9 p.m. Last season's adult all-lift tickets were $55 on weekdays and $82 on weekends. Prices for 2026-27 weren't posted as of mid-November. A replacement for the Windsor chair is funded, with construction set for spring 2027.

Anthony Lakes

Anthony Lakes in the Elkhorn Mountains, near North Powder and Baker City, has a 7,100-foot base, the highest in Oregon, and averages about 250 inches of snow a year. It has one triple chair, a handle tow and a carpet lift, 900 feet of vertical, and is open Thursday through Sunday. Adult day tickets are $55, kids 6 and under ski free, and every Thursday is "Thirty Dollar Thursday." It's run with a nonprofit partner on Wallowa-Whitman National Forest land.

How Much Are Lift Tickets in Oregon?

Adult day tickets at Oregon ski resorts range from about $55 at small hills to more than $160 at the window at Mt. Bachelor and Timberline. Buying online and in advance is the easiest way to save, especially at Mt. Bachelor, where online tickets start at $99. Day-of online tickets there are $25 less than the window rate.

Mt. Hood Snow Tubing and Snow Play

For Mt. Hood snow tubing, go to Skibowl, Summit Pass or Cooper Spur. Mt. Hood Meadows and Timberline's main mountain don't have tubing hills. Resorts supply tubes for a per-person fee.

  • Skibowl Snow Tube and Adventure Park (Government Camp): several tubing runs with conveyor lifts, plus "Cosmic Tubing" with LED lights and lasers on winter weekend and holiday evenings.
  • Summit Pass (Government Camp): a tubing and sledding hill beside the ski area.
  • Cooper Spur: a tubing center with an easier and a steeper hill, each with a rope tow.
  • Bring your own sled: Trillium Lake Sno-Park (3 miles east of Government Camp on Highway 26) and Little John Sno-Park (on Highway 35, about 31 miles south of Hood River) are free apart from the Sno-Park permit.

Elsewhere, try Hoodoo's Autobahn Tubing Park or the weekend tubing hill at Diamond Lake Resort.

Sno-Park Permits, Chains and Winter Driving

From November 1 to April 30, you need an Oregon Sno-Park permit to park in any designated Sno-Park, and that includes many ski area lots such as all of Mt. Hood Meadows' parking. According to Oregon DMV, permits cost:

  • Daily: $4
  • 3-day (consecutive): $9
  • Annual (November 1-April 30): $25

Permit agents, including ski resorts and sporting goods stores, may add a small service fee. Annual permits are sold online through DMV2U, and California and Idaho permits are honored in Oregon. Parking in a Sno-Park without one can bring a $30 fine, according to TripCheck.

Oregon's chain law applies on every highway. When signs say so, you must carry chains or traction tires, and when conditions get worse you must put them on. Passenger vehicles under 10,000 pounds can use approved traction tires instead of chains. Studded tires are legal from November 1 through March 31. Ignoring chain signs is a Class C traffic infraction.

Before any mountain drive, check ODOT TripCheck for Highway 26 and 35 on Mt. Hood, Highway 372 to Mt. Bachelor, Highway 20 over Santiam Pass and Highway 58 to Willamette Pass. Carry food, warm layers and a full tank; storms can close passes for hours.

FAQ

What is the best ski resort in Oregon?

Mt. Bachelor is the top pick for most skiers because of its size (4,323 acres), 9,065-foot summit and drier snow. Mt. Hood Meadows has the most varied terrain near Portland, and Timberline has the longest season.

What ski resorts are closest to Portland, Oregon?

Mt. Hood Skibowl, Summit Pass and Timberline are all about 60 miles from Portland at Government Camp on Highway 26. Mt. Hood Meadows is about 67 miles away.

When does Mt. Bachelor open for 2026-27?

Mt. Bachelor plans to open on December 4, 2026, if there's enough snow. Its 2026-27 winter lift ticket rates run from December 4, 2026, through April 18, 2027.

Can you ski in Oregon in the summer?

Yes. Timberline on Mt. Hood runs lifts on the Palmer Snowfield into the summer, making it one of the only places in North America with lift-served skiing year-round.

Do I need a Sno-Park permit to ski in Oregon?

Often, yes. Sno-Park permits are required in designated Sno-Parks from November 1 to April 30, and many ski area lots are Sno-Parks, including all Mt. Hood Meadows lots and Timberline's state-maintained lots. A day permit is $4 and an annual permit is $25.

What is the cheapest ski resort in Oregon?

Small community hills are cheapest. Cooper Spur ($54 adult) and Anthony Lakes ($55, or $30 on Thursdays) have the lowest regular prices, Mt. Ashland was $55 on weekdays last season, and Willamette Pass advertises advance tickets from $19.
