Moving to Oregon

Your comprehensive relocation guide: utilities, jobs, housing, schools, DMV, and assistance programs by county.

Moving Timeline Checklist

A step-by-step guide to help you relocate smoothly to Oregon.

3–6 Months Before: Research & Budget

Compare cost of living between your current location and Oregon cities. Research neighborhoods, school districts, and employment opportunities. Create a moving budget including transport, deposits, and temporary housing.

2–3 Months Before: Secure Housing & Jobs

Begin apartment hunting or house shopping. Apply for jobs through WorkSource Oregon and Mac's List. Schedule moving company or reserve rental truck. Start decluttering and downsizing belongings.

1 Month Before: Utilities & Documents

Contact utility providers to schedule service activation. Notify employer of move date. Arrange medical record transfers. Gather essential documents: driver's license, vehicle registration, social security card, birth certificates.

2 Weeks Before: Pack & Notify

Pack non-essential items. Update address with banks, creditors, subscription services. Schedule school enrollments for children. Plan pet relocation if applicable. Take photos of valuables and electronics.

1 Week Before: Confirm Everything

Confirm moving dates and times. Purchase packing supplies. Defrost refrigerator. Create an essentials box with toiletries, medications, chargers, and clothes for first few days. Clean old residence if required by lease.

Moving Day & First Week

Arrive at new residence early. Verify utilities are active. Register vehicle at DMV within 30 days. Apply for Oregon Driver's License within 30 days. Enroll children in schools. Locate grocery stores, pharmacies, and healthcare providers.

Utility Setup Guide

Contact these providers before arrival to ensure services are active when you move in.

⚡ Electricity

Main providers vary by region:

  • Portland General Electric (PGE) — Portland Metro, Willamette Valley
  • Pacific Power — Most of Oregon
  • Idaho Power — Eastern Oregon border areas

🔥 Natural Gas

Available in select regions:

  • NW Natural — Portland Metro, Salem, Eugene
  • Avista — Southern Oregon (Medford, Klamath Falls, Roseburg)
  • Cascade Natural Gas — Central & Eastern Oregon (Bend, Pendleton, Ontario)
  • Check local availability—some areas lack gas service

💧 Water & Sewer

Most cities provide water directly through Public Works departments. Contact your city hall or check the utility page on your specific city's website for connection procedures and deposit amounts.

🗑️ Trash & Recycling

Varies by municipality:

  • City-operated services in larger cities
  • Private haulers in suburban/rural areas
  • Contact county waste management for unincorporated areas

📶 Internet & Phone

  • Comcast/Xfinity — Cable
  • Spectrum — Cable
  • CenturyLink — Fiber/DSL
  • Local providers may offer competitive rates

📋 Rent Stabilization

Oregon was the first state with statewide rent stabilization. For buildings more than 15 years old, landlords can raise rent only once a year, by no more than 7% plus inflation (with a hard cap of 10%). Newer buildings are exempt, and after the first year of a tenancy most no-cause evictions are not allowed.

🧾 Property Taxes (Measures 5 & 50)

Oregon taxes homes on an "assessed value" that usually grows only about 3% per year, no matter how fast market prices rise — and that assessed value stays with the home when it sells. Unlike California, where a sale resets the tax base to the purchase price, Oregon buyers often inherit a tax bill well below what the price would suggest. Read more in our guide to moving from California or from the Oregon Department of Revenue.

💰 Cost Considerations

Oregon housing costs vary significantly by region. Portland metro and Bend average higher than rural areas, while Medford, Klamath Falls and eastern Oregon are much more affordable. Effective property tax rates average roughly 0.9% of market value, so also budget for HOA fees where they apply.

Moving to Oregon: Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to get an Oregon driver's license?

You must get an Oregon driver's license within 30 days of becoming a resident, and register your vehicles within the same 30 days. Bring proof of identity, proof of your Oregon address and your out-of-state license to a DMV office. See the Oregon DMV driver's license page.

Does Oregon have a state income tax?

Yes. Oregon's income tax rates range from 4.75% to 9.9%, and residents of the Portland area may also owe regional taxes. In exchange, there is no sales tax. Learn more from the Oregon Department of Revenue, including the "kicker" tax rebate.

Is Oregon a good place to retire?

Many retirees love Oregon's scenery, mild western climate and lack of sales tax, and Social Security benefits are not taxed by the state. However, pensions and retirement account withdrawals are subject to state income tax, and housing can be pricey in Portland and Bend. Medford, Grants Pass and the coast are popular, more affordable choices.

How do I register to vote in Oregon?

Oregon Motor Voter automatically registers most eligible residents when they get an Oregon driver's license or ID at the DMV. You can also register online at sos.oregon.gov. Oregon votes entirely by mail — your ballot arrives at home about two to three weeks before each election.

Which utilities do I need to set up?

Plan to set up electricity (usually Portland General Electric or Pacific Power), natural gas if your home has it (NW Natural, Avista or Cascade Natural Gas), water, sewer and trash through your city, plus internet. Call providers two to three weeks before you move in. See our utility setup guide above.
