Experience the magic of Christmas in Oregon, where the rugged Pacific coastline, snow-capped Cascades, and historic Willamette Valley towns transform into a true winter wonderland. From the world-class light displays in Portland to the European-style holiday markets in Silverton, the Beaver State offers a diverse range of festive traditions for every traveler.

Best Christmas Towns in Oregon

While many cities across the state embrace the holiday spirit, a few select destinations go above and beyond to earn the title of the best Oregon Christmas towns. These locations combine historical charm with extensive community events, making them perfect for a multi-day holiday getaway. Each of these towns offers a distinct "Hallmark movie" vibe, whether it's the Victorian-era storefronts of Jacksonville or the high-desert resort luxury of Sunriver.

Sunriver: A Central Oregon Winter Wonderland

Located just south of Bend, Sunriver Resort is often cited as the premier holiday destination in Central Oregon. The "Traditions at Sunriver" event features a month-long celebration including horse-drawn sleigh rides, outdoor ice skating, and the Grand Illumination tree-lighting ceremony. The resort's landscape, often covered in a pristine layer of High Desert snow, provides a picturesque backdrop for family photos and outdoor winter recreation at nearby Mt. Bachelor. The resort typically hosts gingerbread house contests and holiday-themed dinners throughout December, ensuring that even if you aren't skiing, there is plenty to do.

Ashland: Victorian Charm in Southern Oregon

Ashland transforms during the holidays, leaning into its Victorian architecture and cultural heritage. The annual Festival of Light begins in late November with a grand parade and the illumination of over one million lights. Visitors can enjoy Dickens-themed dinner theater, holiday performances by the Oregon Shakespeare Festival, and winter walks through Lithia Park.

Silverton: Home to the Oregon Garden Christmas Market

Silverton is a major holiday hub thanks to the Silverton Christmas Market. Modeled after traditional German Christkindlmarkts, it features wood-carved huts, artisan vendors, and Bavarian food and drink. The market is famous for its 1.5 million lights and its outdoor ice rink. It is highly recommended to stay at the Oregon Garden Resort to fully immerse in the experience.

Jacksonville: A National Historic Landmark Town

In the Rogue Valley, Jacksonville offers a step back in time. As a National Historic Landmark, its 19th-century buildings are draped in evergreen garlands and red bows. The celebration includes "Jolly Holly Trolley" rides and a Victorian Christmas parade. The town's walkable downtown makes it one of the most romantic spots for an Oregon holiday getaway.

Quick tip: Many of Oregon's most popular holiday events, such as the Shore Acres Holiday Lights and the Silverton Christmas Market, now require timed-entry tickets purchased well in advance. Check official websites in early October to secure your preferred dates and avoid disappointment at the gate.

Top Oregon Christmas Light Displays

If you are looking for the best Christmas light displays in Oregon, the state offers several professional-grade installations that attract visitors from across the Pacific Northwest. These displays vary from religious shrines and botanical gardens to city parks and racetracks.

The Grotto’s Festival of Lights

Located in Northeast Portland, The Grotto’s Festival of Lights is one of the largest choral festivals in the world. The 62-acre Catholic shrine is illuminated with over two million lights, but the real draw is the series of 170 indoor holiday concerts held in the chapel, known for its exceptional acoustics. The serene atmosphere, combined with the walk-through light display, live nativity scenes, and puppet shows, makes it a family favorite. It is a place of reflection as much as celebration, offering a quieter alternative to the high-energy commercial displays elsewhere in the city.

Shore Acres State Park Holiday Lights

On the Southern Oregon Coast, Shore Acres State Park near Coos Bay hosts a legendary light display. The park’s seven-acre botanical garden is draped in over 300,000 LED lights, many shaped like local marine life, such as whales, jellyfish, and sea turtles. Watching the festive lights glow against the backdrop of crashing Pacific waves and rugged cliffs is a uniquely Oregonian experience. Note that parking permits are required and must be reserved through the Oregon State Parks website. According to the Oregon Parks and Recreation Department, this event relies on hundreds of volunteers and has been a coastal tradition since 1987.

Portland ZooLights

A Portland tradition for over three decades, ZooLights at the Oregon Zoo features more than 1.5 million lights. The display includes life-sized animal silhouettes, glowing tunnels, and a colorful light show synchronized to holiday music. Visitors can choose between the traditional walk-through experience or specific drive-through nights for those who prefer to see the lights from the comfort of their vehicle.

Winter Wonderland at PIR

Winter Wonderland at the Portland International Raceway (PIR) is a massive drive-through event. Since 1993, this display has grown to include over 250 colorful light sets and many fully animated scenes. It is an excellent option for rainy nights, allowing you to enjoy millions of lights from your own car.

Magical Holiday Train Rides in Oregon

Are there holiday trains in Oregon? Absolutely. The state’s rich rail history comes alive in November and December with several festive excursions that transport passengers into the pages of a storybook. These trains offer a nostalgic way to view the landscape while enjoying seasonal hospitality.

  • Mt. Hood Railroad (Hood River): The Polar Express Train Ride is a licensed experience based on the classic book and movie. Passengers are encouraged to wear pajamas as they enjoy hot cocoa, cookies, and a visit from Santa. The journey through the Hood River Valley offers stunning views of the surrounding orchards and the shadow of Mt. Hood.
  • Oregon Coast Scenic Railroad (Garibaldi): The Candy Cane Express runs along the beautiful Tillamook Bay. This steam-powered train is decorated with lights and offers a nostalgic way to see the coast while sipping cider and meeting Father Christmas. It is one of the few places in the country where you can ride a vintage steam locomotive along the Pacific shore.
  • Holiday Express (Portland): Operating out of the Oregon Rail Heritage Center, this vintage steam locomotive (often featuring the famous Southern Pacific 4449 or the Spokane, Portland & Seattle 700) takes passengers on a round-trip journey along the Willamette River, offering views of the city skyline and holiday lights.
  • Sumpter Valley Railroad (Baker City/Sumpter): In Eastern Oregon, the Christmas Train offers a rugged, high-elevation holiday experience. Passengers ride in heated vintage cars through the snowy Sumpter Valley, often stopping for a holiday bazaar in the historic gold-mining town of Sumpter.

Unique Oregon Holiday Traditions

While many traditions are universal, Oregon has several unique ways of celebrating the season that reflect its geography, maritime history, and outdoor-oriented culture.

The Christmas Ships Parade

Since 1954, a fleet of private boat owners has decorated their vessels with elaborate light displays to parade down the Willamette and Columbia Rivers. The Christmas Ships Parade is a volunteer-led event that brings thousands of spectators to the riverbanks. There are two separate fleets—one for each river—and they follow different routes nightly for about two weeks in December. Popular viewing spots include the Portland Waterfront, Cathedral Park in St. Johns, and various restaurants with river views in Vancouver and Portland. It is a testament to the region's strong boating community and a free event for the public to enjoy.

The Christmas Paddle in Bend

In a true display of High Desert spirit, Bend residents participate in the annual Christmas Paddle. Dozens of kayakers and paddleboarders decorate their boats with battery-powered lights and festive ornaments to float through the Old Mill District on the Deschutes River. It is a spectacle that perfectly captures the town's love for outdoor adventure. Spectators line the footbridges and riverbanks, often clutching thermoses of hot cider or coffee as the glowing flotilla passes by.

Heceta Head Lighthouse Victorian Christmas

On the central coast, the Heceta Head Lighthouse and its Keeper's House host an annual Victorian Christmas open house. The historic buildings are decorated with period-appropriate ornaments, and local musicians perform holiday classics. It is one of the most atmospheric ways to experience the coast, combining maritime history with the warmth of a traditional Christmas celebration.

Coastal Holiday Traditions and Storm Watching

The Oregon Coast offers a different kind of holiday magic. While you won't find much snow, you will find "storm watching" season in full swing. Many visitors choose to spend Christmas at coastal resorts in Cannon Beach, Seaside, or Newport, where they can watch massive Pacific swells from the safety of a glass-walled lounge. Seaside hosts a popular Parade of Lights, and the Seaside Aquarium often features holiday-themed displays. In Newport, the Sea of Lights at the Oregon Coast Aquarium allows visitors to see the marine exhibits illuminated by thousands of festive lights.

Planning Your Oregon Christmas Getaway

When planning a trip for Christmas in Oregon, it is essential to consider the diverse geography of the state. While the Willamette Valley and Coast generally remain above freezing with average highs in the 40s, the Cascades and Central Oregon experience true winter conditions with temperatures often dropping into the teens.

Travelers heading to Bend, Sunriver, or Joseph should ensure their vehicle is equipped with winter tires or carry chains, as mountain passes like Santiam Pass and Willamette Pass can be treacherous during December storms. The Oregon Department of Transportation (ODOT) provides real-time road conditions via TripCheck.com, which is a vital resource for any holiday traveler. If you prefer to avoid the snow, the Oregon Coast offers a milder (though often rainier) climate, perfect for storm watching by a cozy fireplace. Regardless of where you go, packing layers and waterproof gear is the golden rule for any Oregon winter excursion.

What are the best Christmas towns in Oregon?

The best Christmas towns in Oregon include Sunriver for its resort festivities, Ashland for its Victorian charm and light festival, and Silverton for its world-class German-style holiday market. Jacksonville and Sumpter are also highly regarded for their small-town holiday atmosphere and historic charm.

Where are the best Christmas light displays in Oregon?

Top-rated light displays include The Grotto's Festival of Lights in Portland, the Shore Acres State Park Holiday Lights on the coast, and the Silverton Christmas Market. The Winter Wonderland at Portland International Raceway is also a popular drive-through option for families.

Are there holiday trains in Oregon?

Yes, Oregon has several popular holiday trains. The Mt. Hood Railroad offers the Polar Express, the Oregon Coast Scenic Railroad runs the Candy Cane Express, and the Oregon Rail Heritage Center operates the Holiday Express in Portland. In Eastern Oregon, the Sumpter Valley Railroad also hosts a Christmas Train.

Is there snow in Oregon at Christmas?

Snow is almost guaranteed in the Cascade Mountains and Central Oregon cities like Bend and Sunriver. However, in the Willamette Valley (Portland, Salem, Eugene) and along the Oregon Coast, a "White Christmas" is rare, with rain being far more common during the holiday season. If you want snow, head east of the Cascades.

When do Christmas events start in Oregon?

Most major holiday events in Oregon begin the Friday after Thanksgiving. This includes the lighting of the tree at Pioneer Courthouse Square in Portland and the opening of ZooLights, The Grotto's Festival of Lights, and the Silverton Christmas Market.
