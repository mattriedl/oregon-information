The Draw of the High Desert: Why Everyone is Moving to Bend

Bend, Oregon, situated at the eastern edge of the Cascade Range, offers a lifestyle that is increasingly rare in the modern world. With an estimated population of 107,342 as of July 2025, the city has seen an 8.2% growth since the 2020 Census. People aren't just moving here for the views; they are moving for the sunny, dry high-desert air (Bend averages only about 10.6 inches of precipitation a year) that stands in stark contrast to the misty valleys of Western Oregon.

The city serves as the county seat of Deschutes County and sits at an elevation of about 3,623 feet. This elevation is key to its climate—expect crisp mornings, warm afternoons, and a four-season playground that includes everything from skiing at Mount Bachelor to paddleboarding on the Deschutes River. If you are exploring visiting Oregon before making the leap, Bend is likely at the top of your list.

Bend's transition from a timber-dependent economy to a diverse service-and-tech hub is one of the most successful urban transformations in the West. Today, the city is known as much for its entrepreneurial spirit as it is for its mountains. The presence of Oregon State University-Cascades (OSU-Cascades) has further cemented the city's status as an intellectual and cultural center for Central Oregon.

Moving to Bend Oregon Pros and Cons

Every relocation involves trade-offs, and moving to Bend Oregon pros and cons are distinct. Understanding these before you pack the U-Haul is essential for a successful transition to Central Oregon.

The Pros: Why People Love Bend

The primary draw of Bend is undoubtedly the lifestyle. It is one of the few places in the world where you can realistically ski in the morning and go fly-fishing in the afternoon. The community is deeply rooted in this "work hard, play harder" mentality.

  • Unparalleled Outdoor Access: Whether it's the Deschutes River Trail, with more than 12 miles of path along the river in town, or the mountain bike trails at Phil’s Trail, nature is literally in your backyard. Access to the Three Sisters Wilderness and the Cascade Lakes Highway provides endless weekend adventures.
  • The Brewery Culture: With over 20 craft breweries, Bend has one of the highest breweries-per-capita rates in the country. It's a social pillar of the community, where families and dogs are often as welcome as the patrons.
  • Safety: Bend maintains low crime rates, with violent crime significantly below state and national averages. The feeling of community safety is a major factor for those moving from larger metropolitan areas.
  • Tax Benefits: Like the rest of the state, Bend has no general sales tax, so most purchases ring up at the tag price. Two exceptions: new vehicles carry a 0.5% vehicle privilege or use tax, and new bicycles priced $200 or more carry a $15 excise tax.
  • Economic Vibrancy: Bend has a "can-do" attitude. The local economy is resilient, supported by a mix of healthcare, tourism, and a burgeoning tech sector.

The Cons: The Reality Check

While Bend often looks like a postcard, residents face real-world challenges that come with rapid growth and geographic isolation.

  • Housing Affordability: This is the most significant barrier. The Census puts Bend’s median home value at $718,400 (2020–2024), about 50% higher than Oregon’s $477,600. The average home price in Bend Oregon 2026 has made homeownership a challenge.
  • Wildfire Smoke: From August through September, smoke from regional wildfires can impact air quality and outdoor plans, sometimes for weeks at a time. It requires a "plan B" for indoor activities.
  • US-97 Traffic: The main north-south artery can become a bottleneck. As the city has grown, the infrastructure—particularly the parkway—has struggled to keep up with the volume of local and tourist traffic.
  • The "Bend Bubble": Because the city is so pleasant, it can feel isolated. Accessing major cultural events or international flights often requires a 3-hour trek to Portland.

Cost of Living in Bend, Oregon: Budgeting for the High Desert

The cost of living in Bend Oregon runs well above the national average, mainly because of housing. While Oregon lacks a sales tax, it does have a progressive income tax reaching 9.9%, which you should factor into your Oregon cost of living calculations. For many, the "Sunshine Tax" is a real financial consideration—the idea that you pay a premium to live in such a beautiful, sunny location.

Beyond housing, other costs are more manageable but still reflect a premium. Energy costs can be higher due to the need for heating in the winter and cooling in the summer. Transportation costs are also elevated because of the city's layout and the necessity of owning a vehicle equipped for winter conditions. Snow tires and all-wheel-drive vehicles are practically standard equipment for anyone living here year-round.

Quick tip: When calculating your budget, don't forget to account for seasonal gear. Living in Bend means you'll likely want a mountain bike, skis or a snowboard, a kayak, and hiking boots. These "recreation taxes" can add up, but they are the key to enjoying everything the region has to offer.

The Remote Work Capital: Remote Work in Bend Oregon Guide

A remote work in Bend Oregon guide wouldn't be complete without mentioning that about 28% of Bend’s workers worked from home in 2023, according to the City of Bend’s 2025 State of the Economy report (Census ACS data). That is one of the highest rates in the country, earning Bend the nickname of the "remote work capital." The shift toward remote work has fundamentally changed the city's demographic, bringing in high-earning professionals from larger tech hubs.

Infrastructure for remote workers is robust. Coworking spaces like BendTECH and The Haven provide professional environments for those who don't want to work from their living rooms every day. These spaces often host networking events, helping to bridge the gap between "working from home" and "working in a community."

Additionally, the proximity to Redmond Municipal Airport – Roberts Field (RDM), about 17 miles north, allows for easy travel to tech hubs. Direct flights to Seattle, San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Denver make it possible to maintain a remote role with a company based elsewhere while enjoying the lifestyle of Central Oregon.

Best Neighborhoods in Bend Oregon for Families

Finding the right spot depends on your lifestyle and budget. The best neighborhoods in Bend Oregon for families often prioritize proximity to parks and the highly-regarded Bend-La Pine Schools.

NorthWest Crossing: The Gold Standard

Located on the Westside, NorthWest Crossing is a sustainably designed community where you can walk to schools, restaurants, and shops. It’s highly sought after for its community feel and active vibe. The neighborhood features its own "town center" with coffee shops and eateries. Expect to pay a premium over the average home price in Bend Oregon 2026 here, and check recent neighborhood sales before you budget.

Southeast Bend: The Best Value

For those looking for more house for their dollar, Southeast Bend and the Old Farm District offer newer subdivisions and larger lots. Pine Nursery Park, a 159-acre park with trails, sports fields and a big off-leash dog area, is a short drive north in Northeast Bend. Southeast Bend is a good place to start if you’re shopping for an entry-level home in Deschutes County.

Awbrey Butte: Views and Privacy

If you're looking for iconic Bend views, Awbrey Butte is the place. Set on a butte northwest of downtown, this neighborhood features custom-built homes on large, tiered lots. It's a favorite for retirees and families who want a bit more privacy and don't mind a short drive to reach the city's amenities.

Education and Schools in Bend

The Bend-La Pine School District serves over 16,500 students and consistently receives high marks for both academic performance and extracurricular opportunities. Schools like Summit High School and Highland Magnet at Kenwood are particularly well-regarded. If you are moving to Oregon with children, the quality of schools in Bend is often the deciding factor.

The district is known for its innovative approach, including several magnet schools and a strong emphasis on outdoor and environmental education. Programs vary from school to school, so ask each school you’re considering what outdoor learning it offers.

Transportation and Getting Around

While Bend is increasingly bike-friendly, it remains a city where a car is largely necessary. The Cascades East Transit (CET) system provides bus service, but frequencies can be low. For daily life, most residents rely on their own vehicles equipped for winter conditions.

Bend has about 50 roundabouts, which keep traffic moving at many intersections that would otherwise need signals. Parking in the downtown and Old Mill District can be challenging during summer weekends. Many residents choose to "e-bike" into town to avoid the hassle, and e-bikes have become a common sight around town.

Healthcare and Quality of Life

Healthcare in Bend is anchored by the St. Charles Health System, which operates the largest hospital in the region. St. Charles Bend is a Level II Trauma Center and provides a wide range of specialty services. This robust medical infrastructure is a major draw for retirees and those with specific health needs.

The quality of life in Bend is consistently ranked among the highest in the nation. This isn't just because of the mountains; it's the culture. People here are generally friendly, active, and engaged in their community. It's a place where people value their time as much as their bank accounts, creating a balanced and fulfilling environment.

Economic Landscape and Job Market

While remote work is a major driver, the local economy is anchored by health care, tourism, and education. The median household income is approximately $96,394, which is about 19% higher than the national median. However, most buyers need a household income well above that median to comfortably afford the current average home price in Bend Oregon 2026.

The tech sector is also growing, with several home-grown success stories in software and outdoor gear industries. If you're looking for more affordable alternatives within the region, Oregon's small towns like Redmond offer a similar high-desert vibe at a lower price point.

Is Bend Right for You?

Ultimately, living in Bend is a lifestyle choice. If you value being able to mountain bike after work, enjoy a local IPA with friends, and don't mind the high cost of entry, it's hard to find a better place in the West. The city continues to evolve, balancing its rapid growth with a commitment to maintaining the natural beauty that drew people here in the first place. Is it expensive to live in Bend, Oregon?

Yes, mostly because of housing. The Census puts Bend’s median home value at $718,400 (2020–2024), about 50% above Oregon’s, and most buyers need a household income well above the local median of $96,394.

What is the weather like in Bend?

Bend has a dry high-desert climate, with only about 10.6 inches of precipitation a year. Winters are cold, with an average of about 22 inches of snow in town (NOAA 1991–2020 normals). Summers are warm with very low humidity.

Are schools good in Bend, Oregon?

Bend-La Pine Schools serves about 16,700 students and runs three magnet schools. Ratings vary by school, so check each school’s state report card and visit before you choose a neighborhood.

How far is Bend from Portland?

Bend is approximately 160 miles southeast of Portland, typically a 3 to 3.5-hour drive via US-97 and US-26 over Mount Hood, or via US-20 and OR-22 through Salem, depending on mountain pass conditions.

What is the average rent in Bend, Oregon?

The Census puts Bend’s median gross rent at $1,883 (2020–2024), and Zillow’s average rent was about $2,284 in fall 2026. Newer apartments and single-family rentals usually cost more.
