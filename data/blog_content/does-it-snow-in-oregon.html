Does It Snow in Oregon? It Depends on Elevation

Yes, it snows in Oregon every winter, but most of it falls in the mountains, not in the cities where most people live. The Cascade Range splits the state in two. Moist Pacific storms hit the west side first and drop rain in the valleys and huge amounts of snow above roughly 3,000 to 4,000 feet. East of the Cascades the air is colder and drier, so towns like Bend, Pendleton and Joseph see snow more often, but in smaller amounts than the passes.

The numbers in this guide come mainly from the NOAA NCEI 1991–2020 U.S. Climate Normals, the official 30-year averages for each weather station, plus the National Weather Service's climate books for Portland, Salem and Eugene. Averages hide a lot of swing: in the western valleys, many winters bring a dusting or nothing, and an occasional big storm pulls the average up.

The rough pattern by region:

  • Portland metro and the Willamette Valley: a few inches a year, often less, plus the odd ice storm.
  • Oregon Coast: almost never at sea level. The Coast Range passes on Highways 26, 6, 18 and 20 can get snow.
  • Central Oregon: regular, drier snow.
  • Eastern Oregon: modest in the valleys, heavy in mountain towns.
  • Southern Oregon: little in the Rogue Valley, a lot on Siskiyou Pass and at Crater Lake.

Does It Snow in Portland, Oregon?

Yes, but not much: Portland averages about 4.2 inches of snow a year, and in a typical winter there are only one or two days with an inch or more. Those are the 1991–2020 NOAA normals for Portland International Airport, which also show snow covering the ground (at least an inch) on fewer than three days a year.

Real storms do happen. According to the National Weather Service Portland climate book, the airport's snowiest winter on record was 1949–50, with 44.5 inches, including 41.4 inches in January 1950 alone. More recently, February 22, 2023 brought 10.8 inches, the biggest one-day February snowfall in the airport record. The February 2021 storm, pictured here, also buried much of the city.

Local quirks for anyone in Multnomah County:

  • Elevation matters. Neighborhoods in the West Hills, on Mount Tabor or up toward Sylvan often get snow when the flats near the river only get rain.
  • East wind from the Columbia River Gorge pulls cold air into the city. When moist air rides over that cold layer, you get freezing rain, which coats roads, trees and power lines in ice. That's what happened in January 2024, when an ice storm left thousands of Oregonians without power (OPB).
  • The city slows down fast. With so few snow days, many drivers don't have traction tires, steep streets get slick quickly, and schools often close after just a couple of inches.

Does It Snow in Salem and Eugene?

Yes, a little: Salem and Eugene each average about 6 inches of snow a year over the long run, usually spread across a couple of days. The National Weather Service's Eugene climate book lists a 1991–2020 snowfall normal of 6.1 inches, with January the snowiest month. Salem's long-term average is about 6.5 inches (1892–1995, the years with full snow records, per the Salem climate book). Corvallis, in between, averages 2.9 inches.

Valley snow usually melts within a day or two. The exceptions are memorable: Eugene's biggest 24-hour snowfall was 22.9 inches on January 25–26, 1969, leaving 34 inches on the ground. More recently, Eugene got 8.2 inches on December 26, 2021, its snowiest December day on record.

Hoping for a white Christmas? Don't count on it in the valley. The Salem climate book puts the chance of snow on Christmas at about 1%. For a better shot, head to the mountains or east of the Cascades (see our Christmas in Oregon guide).

Does It Snow in Bend and Central Oregon?

Yes: Bend averages about 21.6 inches of snow a year in town, with about eight days a year bringing an inch or more. At about 3,660 feet, Bend sits in the dry rain shadow of the Cascades, so storms are smaller than on the mountain, but cold nights help snow stick around. Snow can fall any time from November through March, and occasionally in October or April.

Lower, drier towns in and around Deschutes County get less: Madras averages about 14.1 inches and Prineville about 8.8 inches.

The real snow is 22 miles west of town at Mt. Bachelor, one of the biggest ski areas in the country. If you're thinking about relocating, our guide to moving to Bend covers what winter is actually like to live with, and our Oregon ski resorts guide has opening dates and lift prices.

Does It Snow on the Oregon Coast or in Southern Oregon?

Snow on the Oregon coast is rare: NOAA's 1991–2020 averages are 0.0 inches at Seaside, 0.2 inches at Tillamook and 0.9 inches at Yachats. Coastal winters mean rain, wind and big surf instead (see our Oregon coast storm watching guide). The catch is getting there: the Coast Range highways from Portland and the valley can get snow and ice even when the beaches are wet and 45 degrees.

Southern Oregon's valleys are similar. Medford averages about 3.4 inches a year, Grants Pass about 0.9 inches and Ashland about half an inch at its in-town station. Just south of Ashland, though, Interstate 5 climbs over Siskiyou Pass (4,310 feet), the highest point on I-5. ODOT says conditional closures, when traction tires aren't enough and chains are required, are frequent there in winter. Up in the mountains, Howard Prairie Dam east of Ashland averages about 118 inches, and Crater Lake gets more snow than almost anywhere in the state.

How Much Snow Does Eastern Oregon Get?

Eastern Oregon gets more frequent snow than the western valleys, but totals range from under 10 inches in the river valleys to more than 50 inches in mountain towns. Cold air settles in the basins east of the Cascades, so snow that falls tends to stay on the ground longer.

  • Pendleton averages about 15.7 inches a year at its airport, with about 13 days a year with at least an inch of snow on the ground.
  • La Grande averages about 9.4 inches, but nearby Interstate 84 over the Blue Mountains (Cabbage Hill and Ladd Canyon) is notorious for winter closures.
  • Joseph, at about 4,200 feet below the Wallowa Mountains, averages about 54 inches.
  • Lakeview, one of Oregon's highest towns at nearly 4,800 feet, averages about 55 inches.
  • In the Columbia River Gorge, Hood River averages about 17 inches, while The Dalles, farther east and drier, averages about 5.5 inches.

When Does It Snow in Oregon?

In Portland and the Willamette Valley, snow almost always falls between December and February, with January the most likely month. In the mountains, snow season runs from November into April or May. The Portland climate book shows a normal of one day with measurable snow in December, two in January and one in February, and almost none in November or March.

  • November: the Cascades and Blue Mountains usually get their first lasting snow. Valley snow is unusual; Portland's earliest measurable snow on record came November 11, 1955.
  • December: valley snow becomes possible, especially with an east wind.
  • January: the coldest and snowiest month for most Oregon cities.
  • February: still a real chance of a valley snowstorm, as in 2019, 2021 and 2023.
  • March and April: spring snow in the mountains and occasional flurries elsewhere. Portland's latest measurable snow on record fell April 11–12, 2022.

Snow levels decide everything. National Weather Service forecasts give a "snow level" in feet, the elevation where rain turns to snow. When it falls to near the valley floor, Portland and the valley can see snow; when it stays several thousand feet up, only the hills and passes do. Will It Snow in Portland This Winter (2026–27)?

No one can forecast a specific snowstorm months ahead, but this winter's strong El Niño tilts the odds toward a warmer winter with less valley snow than normal. In its October 15, 2026 outlook, NOAA's Climate Prediction Center said a very strong El Niño is in place and is favored to last through spring 2027. Its November–January outlook favors above-normal temperatures for the Pacific Northwest and above-normal precipitation along the West Coast as far north as the Oregon coast.

What that means for snow: Oregon State Climatologist Larry O'Neill told OPB that this "super El Niño" could bring a lot of precipitation, but most of it is expected to fall as rain rather than snow because of warmer temperatures. He also noted that last winter brought Oregon's worst snowpack on record, so even a below-normal year could beat it.

A warm winter can still include one cold snap, and one arctic outbreak with an east wind is all it takes for a Portland snow day. The outlook is updated monthly, so keep checking it and your local National Weather Service forecast.

Where to Find Snow on Purpose

If you want to see snow in Oregon, drive to it: Mt. Hood is about 60 miles from Portland, Santiam Pass and Willamette Pass are roughly 70 to 85 miles from Salem and Eugene, and Mt. Bachelor is 22 miles from Bend. Most mountain parking lots are state Sno-Parks, so you need a permit from November 1 through April 30 ($4 a day or $25 for the season; see TripCheck's Sno-Park page). Two classic snow trips:

Government Camp and Mt. Hood

Government Camp, the village on US 26 below Timberline Lodge, averages more than 230 inches of snow a year. It's the closest reliable snow to Portland, with sledding and tubing, Nordic trails, snowshoeing and five ski areas on the mountain.

Crater Lake National Park in winter

Crater Lake's headquarters averages about 463 inches of snow a year, and the National Park Service calls it one of the snowiest inhabited places in the USA. The park stays open year-round, but Rim Drive and the North Entrance Road close to cars for the season with the first big snowstorm or on November 1, whichever comes first, and Rim Drive becomes a ski and snowshoe route (NPS operating hours).

Oregon Chains, Traction Tires and Winter Driving Rules

Oregon's chain law applies to every highway in the state: when signs say chains are required, passenger vehicles under 10,000 pounds that aren't towing can use traction tires instead. That's how ODOT explains it on TripCheck's chain law page. The key points:

  • "Carry chains or traction tires" means you must have chains or traction tires in or on your vehicle, sized to fit.
  • "Chains required" means vehicles towing or rated over 10,000 pounds must chain up. Cars and light trucks that aren't towing must use chains or traction tires.
  • Traction tires are studded tires or tires marked with the three-peak mountain and snowflake symbol. Check your sidewall for the symbol before winter (TripCheck traction tires).
  • Studded tires are legal only from November 1 through March 31. ODOT asks drivers to use them only when needed because they damage roads.
  • Conditional closures can require chains on all vehicles regardless of tires, or all but four-wheel drive. They're frequent on I-5 over Siskiyou Pass.

Before any winter trip over the Cascades, the Coast Range or the Blue Mountains, check road cameras and conditions on TripCheck or call 511. Carry chains even with all-wheel drive, plus water, a blanket and a full tank. New to Oregon? Our Moving to Oregon guide covers more about settling in.

Does It Snow in Oregon? Frequently Asked Questions

Does it snow in Portland, Oregon every year?

No. Portland averages about 4.2 inches a year, but many winters bring only a trace or nothing at all. Average years usually include one or two days with an inch or more of snow, while a handful of winters bring a major storm that shuts down the city.

What month does it snow in Oregon?

In Portland and the Willamette Valley, January is the snowiest month, followed by December and February. In the Cascades and eastern Oregon, snow usually starts in November and can last into April, with mountain snow sometimes falling in May.

Which part of Oregon gets the most snow?

The Cascade Mountains. Crater Lake National Park's headquarters averages about 463 inches a year and Government Camp on Mt. Hood about 233 inches. Among towns, mountain communities like Lakeview and Joseph average more than 50 inches.

Does it snow on the Oregon coast?

Rarely. NOAA's 1991–2020 averages show less than an inch a year in coastal towns such as Seaside, Tillamook, Yachats and Bandon. Snow is more likely on the Coast Range highways you drive to reach the beaches.

Do you need snow tires in Portland?

Most Portlanders get by without them, because snow days are rare. If you drive to the mountains, over the passes or to Bend in winter, you'll need traction tires or chains, and studded tires are legal only from November 1 through March 31.

Is it going to be a snowy winter in Oregon in 2026–27?

A strong El Niño is in place, and NOAA's outlook favors a warmer-than-normal winter in the Pacific Northwest. Oregon's state climatologist expects much of the precipitation to fall as rain, so a below-normal snow year is more likely than not, though a single cold snap can still bring valley snow.
