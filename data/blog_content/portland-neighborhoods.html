Portland has about 95 officially recognized neighborhoods, and each one feels like a small town of its own. This guide compares the best neighborhoods in Portland, Oregon for families, young professionals, retirees, buyers on a budget and visitors, so you can figure out where to live in Portland (or where to stay for a weekend) before you sign a lease or book a hotel.

How Portland Neighborhoods Are Organized

Before you compare individual Portland neighborhoods, learn the map. Burnside Street divides the city north from south, and the Willamette River divides east from west. Every address starts with one of six "sextants": North (N), Northeast (NE), Northwest (NW), Southeast (SE), Southwest (SW) and South (S), the newest, which covers the riverfront area south of downtown, including South Waterfront.

  • Northwest & the Pearl: dense, urban and very walkable, with condos, lofts, boutiques and Forest Park on the doorstep.
  • Southwest: hilly and green, with larger lots, the Oregon Zoo and Washington Park, plus OHSU and quick access to the Westside suburbs.
  • Southeast: the classic "Portland" streetcar neighborhoods (Hawthorne, Belmont, Division, Sellwood) full of bungalows, cafes and food carts.
  • Northeast: historic districts (Irvington, Laurelhurst, Alameda) mixed with lively corridors like Alberta Street.
  • North: the "fifth quadrant" peninsula, home to Mississippi Avenue, Kenton and St. Johns, with a more independent, small-town feel.

Portland's short 200-foot blocks make most close-in neighborhoods easy to walk, and the city's planning goal of "20-minute neighborhoods" (daily needs within a 20-minute walk or bike ride) explains why so many areas have their own main street. Best Neighborhoods in Portland for Families

Families usually want three things: safe residential streets, parks within walking distance and a strong neighborhood school. These best neighborhoods in Portland for families check all three boxes.

Laurelhurst

Laurelhurst straddles Northeast and Southeast Portland and is one of the city's most sought-after addresses. Its curving, tree-lined streets are full of Craftsman, Colonial Revival and Tudor homes, and the gilded Joan of Arc statue at Coe Circle is a local landmark. Laurelhurst Park, with its spring-fed pond, mature trees and playgrounds, works as everyone's backyard. Homes rarely sit on the market long and are among the most expensive on the east side.

Sellwood-Moreland

Sellwood-Moreland in Southeast Portland feels like a self-contained village. Its main streets, SE 13th Avenue (known for "Antique Row") and SE Milwaukie Avenue, are lined with cafes, bakeries and family-owned shops. Kids love Oaks Amusement Park, a historic riverside park that has been open since 1905. Nature lovers have Oaks Bottom Wildlife Refuge and Sellwood Riverfront Park, and the paved Springwater Corridor trail connects the neighborhood to downtown by bike.

Eastmoreland

Next door to Sellwood, Eastmoreland is quiet and leafy, with large older homes, wide streets and very little through traffic. It's home to Reed College, the public Eastmoreland Golf Course and the Crystal Springs Rhododendron Garden. If you want a calm, established neighborhood that's still about 15 minutes from downtown, it's hard to beat. The MAX Orange Line stop at SE Bybee Boulevard gives you a fast train into the city.

Irvington

Irvington, in close-in Northeast Portland, is a National Register historic district known for its grand Craftsman, Queen Anne and Colonial Revival homes. Families like its sidewalks, its quiet grid of shady streets and its location next to NE Broadway, the Lloyd District and Grant Park.

Multnomah Village & Southwest Portland

Southwest Portland trades the east side's flat grid for hills, forests and bigger lots. Multnomah Village has a walkable main street with an independent bookstore, pubs and shops, while nearby Hillsdale, Bridlemile and Hayhurst are quiet, residential and close to Gabriel Park. Homes here are well placed for commuting to OHSU, downtown and the Westside tech corridor.

Best Portland Neighborhoods for Young Professionals

Portland's median household income is about $89,000 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey). The city's job base spans athletic apparel (Nike, Adidas and Columbia Sportswear all have major operations in the metro), healthcare (OHSU, Providence, Legacy) and the semiconductor "Silicon Forest" in Hillsboro. These are the best Portland neighborhoods for young professionals who want nightlife, transit and a short commute.

The Pearl District

Once a rail yard and warehouse district, the Pearl is now Portland's most polished urban neighborhood, full of converted lofts, mid-rise condos, art galleries and some of the city's best restaurants. Powell's City of Books, the world's largest independent new-and-used bookstore, sits on its southern edge. Jamison Square's splash fountain and the restored wetland at Tanner Springs Park add green space. The Portland Streetcar runs right through the neighborhood, and many residents live without a car.

Northwest District: Nob Hill & Slabtown

Just west of the Pearl, the Northwest District (often called Nob Hill) centers on NW 23rd and NW 21st Avenues, two of Portland's best shopping and dining streets. Victorian homes and older apartment buildings line the side streets, and Slabtown on the north end adds newer apartments and grocery stores. Best of all, Forest Park, one of the largest urban forests in the country at more than 5,000 acres, starts at the end of the street.

Boise-Mississippi

N Mississippi Avenue in the Boise neighborhood mixes indie retailers, brewpubs, food-cart pods and live-music venues like Mississippi Studios. It feels less polished than the Pearl, with more independent businesses. The N Williams/Vancouver bike corridor gets commuters downtown in about 15 minutes.

Division & Richmond

SE Division Street has become one of the city's busiest restaurant streets, with a dense row of acclaimed restaurants, bars, coffee roasters and ice cream shops. The surrounding Richmond neighborhood has a mix of new apartments and classic bungalows, and frequent bus service downtown.

Tip: Check the bike score as well as the walk score when house hunting. Portland has hundreds of miles of bikeways and neighborhood greenways, and in close-in areas like Boise, Sunnyside and Richmond, biking downtown is often as fast as driving at rush hour.

Most Walkable Neighborhoods in Portland

If you want to live car-light, focus on the neighborhoods closest to the city center and its main streetcar corridors:

  1. Pearl District: groceries, parks, restaurants and the Streetcar within a few blocks.
  2. Downtown: every MAX line, Pioneer Courthouse Square and the Waterfront Park trail.
  3. Northwest District: two shopping streets plus trail access to Forest Park.
  4. Hawthorne & Belmont: the east side's most walkable main streets, with vintage shops, the historic Bagdad Theater and Belmont's cafes.
  5. Buckman & Kerns: close to the Central Eastside's breweries, restaurants and Lower Burnside.
  6. Alberta Arts District: galleries, murals and the "Last Thursday" street art walk held in summer.

Most Affordable Neighborhoods in Portland

Looking for the cheapest neighborhoods in Portland that are still pleasant to live in? These areas usually cost less than close-in Southeast or Northwest Portland:

St. Johns

At the tip of the North Portland peninsula, St. Johns was once its own city and still feels like a small town. Its walkable downtown on N Lombard Street has a movie theater, a seasonal farmers market and plenty of local bars. Cathedral Park, beneath the Gothic towers of the 1931 St. Johns Bridge, is one of the most photographed spots in Portland. It's a long-time favorite of first-time buyers.

Montavilla

Montavilla, along SE Stark Street east of Mt. Tabor, has a growing main street with the second-run Academy Theater, a Sunday farmers market and a crop of new restaurants. It's still mostly modest bungalows and mid-century homes, priced below Hawthorne or Laurelhurst.

Foster-Powell, Lents & Gateway

Farther out in Southeast and East Portland, Foster-Powell, Lents and Gateway are some of the city's most diverse and affordable neighborhoods, with easy MAX Green Line access and the Springwater Corridor trail. Their trade-offs are longer commutes and, in some pockets, higher property-crime rates, so tour blocks individually before you buy.

Safest Neighborhoods in Portland

Many people search for the safest neighborhoods in Portland after seeing national headlines, so start with recent data. The City of Portland reported that homicides fell 51% in the first half of 2025 compared with the same period a year earlier. Portland also had the largest drop in violent crime among the 68 agencies in the Major Cities Chiefs Association's midyear report.

Crime in Portland is concentrated in a few areas. Quiet, low-density residential neighborhoods such as Eastmoreland, Laurelhurst, Alameda, Sellwood-Moreland, Bridlemile, Hayhurst and Multnomah consistently see less crime than the city center. The most common issues citywide are property crimes like car break-ins, catalytic converter theft and bike theft, so a garage and a good U-lock are worth having anywhere. Before signing a lease, check a specific address on the Portland Police Bureau's open data crime dashboards for a block-by-block view.

Portland Neighborhoods with the Best Schools

Portland Public Schools (PPS) is Oregon's largest school district, with about 42,600 students in fall 2025. Neighborhoods often associated with high-performing PPS schools include Alameda, Laurelhurst, Irvington, Eastmoreland and parts of Southwest Portland (the Wilson/Ida B. Wells High School area). PPS is currently reviewing school boundaries and building use, so always confirm the assigned school for a specific address with the district's boundary tool before you buy.

Many families looking for the Portland neighborhoods with the best school districts also consider nearby suburbs. Lake Oswego and West Linn are known for some of the highest-rated districts in Oregon, and Happy Valley (North Clackamas School District) is a fast-growing family suburb.

Where to Stay in Portland as a Visitor

"Where to stay in Portland" is searched even more than where to live. These are the best neighborhoods to stay in Portland for a first visit:

  • Downtown: best for first-timers. The MAX Red Line runs straight from Portland International Airport (PDX) to downtown in about 40 minutes, and you can walk to Pioneer Courthouse Square, Portland Saturday Market and the Tom McCall Waterfront Park.
  • Pearl District: boutique hotels, top restaurants and Powell's City of Books, all a short walk or streetcar ride from downtown.
  • Nob Hill / NW 23rd: charming inns and rentals, great shopping and easy trail access to Forest Park and Washington Park (home of the International Rose Test Garden and the Portland Japanese Garden).
  • Lloyd District / Convention Center: on the east side next to the Oregon Convention Center and Moda Center, with direct MAX service to the airport and downtown. Good value for events.
  • Central Eastside, Hawthorne & Mississippi: boutique hotels and vacation rentals for travelers who want to experience Portland's food carts, breweries and coffee shops like a local.

Parks and the Outdoors Near Portland Neighborhoods

Access to nature is one of the main reasons people move to Portland. Mt. Tabor, a neighborhood built around an extinct volcanic cinder cone in Southeast Portland, has wooded trails, historic reservoirs and sunset views of downtown. In the West Hills, Forest Park's 30-mile Wildwood Trail starts minutes from Northwest neighborhoods like Hillside and Linnton. On the east side, the Springwater Corridor connects Sellwood, Lents and Gresham by bike.

Best Suburbs of Portland

If none of these city neighborhoods fits, consider the best places to live near Portland:

  • Lake Oswego: an upscale lake town with top schools, about 20 minutes south of downtown.
  • Beaverton and Hillsboro: the Silicon Forest, home to Nike's world headquarters and Intel's largest campuses, served by the MAX Blue Line.
  • Tigard: central Washington County with easy access to I-5 and Highway 217.
  • Milwaukie: a small downtown on the MAX Orange Line, right next to Sellwood.
  • Gresham: some of the metro's most affordable homes, at the east end of the MAX Blue Line.

Frequently Asked Questions About Portland Neighborhoods

What is the best neighborhood to live in Portland, Oregon?

It depends on your priorities. Sellwood-Moreland and Laurelhurst are top picks for families, the Pearl District and Northwest District are best for walkable urban living, and Hawthorne/Richmond offers the classic eclectic Portland lifestyle. St. Johns and Montavilla are the best values.

What are the safest neighborhoods in Portland?

Low-density residential neighborhoods such as Eastmoreland, Laurelhurst, Alameda, Sellwood-Moreland and Southwest Portland's Bridlemile, Hayhurst and Multnomah areas generally have the lowest crime rates. Check the Portland Police Bureau's open data dashboards for any specific address.

Which Portland neighborhood is best for families?

Laurelhurst, Eastmoreland, Sellwood-Moreland, Irvington, Alameda and Multnomah Village are favorites with families thanks to their parks, quiet streets and well-regarded neighborhood schools.

What are the most walkable neighborhoods in Portland?

The Pearl District, Downtown and the Northwest District (Nob Hill) are the most walkable, followed by Hawthorne, Belmont, Buckman and the Alberta Arts District on the east side.

What is the cheapest neighborhood to live in Portland?

Outer Southeast and East Portland neighborhoods such as Lents, Foster-Powell, Gateway and Parkrose are generally the most affordable. Montavilla and St. Johns offer a good balance of price, character and walkable main streets.

Where should I stay in Portland for the first time?

Downtown is the best area for first-time visitors because the MAX Red Line connects it directly to the airport and the main sights are walkable. The Pearl District and Nob Hill are great alternatives if you want boutique hotels and top restaurants nearby.

Is the Pearl District a good place to live?

Yes, for people who want a walkable, urban lifestyle. The Pearl has groceries, parks, galleries and restaurants within a few blocks, plus Streetcar and MAX access. Expect higher condo prices and HOA fees than in east-side neighborhoods.

How many neighborhoods are in Portland?

Portland has about 95 officially recognized neighborhoods, organized into six address sextants: North, Northeast, Northwest, Southeast, Southwest and South.

Are Portland schools good?

Portland Public Schools has many highly rated neighborhood schools, especially in Alameda, Laurelhurst, Irvington, Eastmoreland and Southwest Portland. Suburban districts such as Lake Oswego and West Linn-Wilsonville are among the highest-rated in Oregon.

Portland rewards exploration. Spend a weekend walking a few main streets (SE Hawthorne, NW 23rd, N Mississippi, SE 13th in Sellwood) and you'll quickly feel which neighborhood fits you.

Information is for general guidance. Confirm school boundaries, crime statistics and current home prices with official sources and local agents before making a moving decision.
