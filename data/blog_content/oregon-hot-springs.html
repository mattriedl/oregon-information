Oregon sits on a chain of volcanoes and fault-block deserts, and that heat comes to the surface in dozens of places. The best Oregon hot springs range from cedar-tub bathhouses in old-growth forest to terraced cliffside pools above a river and big mineral ponds out in the high desert. This guide covers the hot springs worth the drive near Portland, Eugene, Bend and in eastern Oregon, with 2026 fees, hours, winter access, the rules for each site and which ones need a reservation.

Quick answer: Oregon hot springs at a glance

  • Closest to Portland: Bagby Hot Springs (wild, $5 cash, 1.5-mile hike) and Breitenbush (retreat center, day pass from $40, reservations required).
  • Closest to Eugene: Terwilliger (Cougar) Hot Springs, $12 per person for 2 hours, and Belknap Hot Springs Resort, $12 per person for one hour.
  • Most famous wild soak: Umpqua Hot Springs, terraced pools above the North Umpqua River ($5 per vehicle, cashless).
  • Best desert soaks: Summer Lake Hot Springs (overnight guests only), Crane Hot Springs near Burns and Alvord Hot Springs below Steens Mountain.
  • Winter: most springs stay open, but Forest Service roads are not plowed. Chains, a full tank and a backup plan are part of the trip.

What Kinds of Hot Springs Does Oregon Have?

Oregon's hot springs fall into three groups: wild pools on national forest land, private resorts with lodging, and retreat-style centers that sell day passes by reservation.

  • Wild or primitive springs such as Bagby, Terwilliger and Umpqua are on Forest Service land. You park at a trailhead, walk in, pay a small fee and soak in rock or wooden pools.
  • Resorts such as Belknap, Crane and Hot Lake Springs pipe mineral water into pools or ponds and rent rooms, cabins and RV sites.
  • Retreat centers such as Breitenbush and Summer Lake are quieter, with rules on alcohol, noise and day use. Book ahead, often weeks ahead.

Geography matters too. The western Cascades springs sit in deep, wet forest and are a short drive from the Willamette Valley. The desert springs of eastern Oregon are hours from any city, with huge skies and very few people.

What Are the Best Hot Springs Near Portland?

The two hot springs closest to Portland are Bagby Hot Springs in the Mt. Hood National Forest and Breitenbush Hot Springs near Detroit. Both take about two hours or more from Portland.

Bagby Hot Springs

Bagby is the classic Portland-area soak: hollowed cedar-log tubs in wooden bathhouses at the end of an old-growth forest trail. The Forest Service says the site is about 40 miles southeast of Estacada, reached by OR 224 and Forest Roads 46, 63 and 70, then Trail #544, about 1.5 miles each way. The main bathhouse spring flows at 138°F.

The site is run by a concessionaire, Bagby Preservation. The fee is $5 per person, cash only at the trailhead, or you can pay at the Ripplebrook Store when it is open. Passes are not accepted, there is no reservation system and weekend waits can be long. When you finish, drain your tub and refill it halfway so it has time to cool for the next person.

2026 note: the Austin Fire closed Bagby and parts of the Clackamas River corridor in late summer 2026. As of early November the Forest Service listed Bagby as open, and the reduced fire closure order that runs through December 31, 2026 no longer named Bagby. Check the Mt. Hood National Forest fire closure page and TripCheck before you drive.

Breitenbush Hot Springs

Breitenbush is a retreat and conference center 9.1 miles up Forest Road 46 from Detroit, with meadow pools and a sauna at roughly 100 to 110°F. Soaking areas are clothing optional; clothes are required in the lodge and parking lot. It is alcohol-free with no cell service.

According to the Breitenbush day-use page, 2026 day passes are $40 on weekdays and $50 on weekends for ages 13 and up, $20 for ages 5 to 12 and free for children 4 and under, with optional lunch. Day use runs 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. and must be booked ahead; passes often sell out weeks in advance and are non-refundable.

Breitenbush is in a snow zone. From November 1 to April 1, carry chains or use traction tires. In 2026 the center said the northern route from Estacada was closed by wildfire, so access is through Detroit on OR 22.

Which Hot Springs Are Near Eugene?

Terwilliger (Cougar) Hot Springs and Belknap Hot Springs Resort are the two main soaks near Eugene, both off the McKenzie Highway (OR 126) east of town. From Eugene and Springfield, they make an easy day trip.

Terwilliger (Cougar) Hot Springs

Terwilliger is about 53 miles east of Eugene in the Willamette National Forest. From OR 126 at Blue River, drive 4 miles east to the Aufderheide Scenic Byway (Forest Road 19), then about 5 miles south past Cougar Reservoir. A quarter-mile walk on the Rider Creek Trail leads to four rock-lined pools that step down a ravine, from about 85°F in the lowest pool to 112°F in the top one.

The Forest Service page lists a fee of $12 per person for 2 hours (ages 12 and up), and no passes are honored. It is day use only, open sunrise to 10 p.m. and closed overnight. The springs are clothing optional, but alcohol and pets are not allowed, and parking is limited, so arrive early on weekends.

Belknap Hot Springs Resort

Belknap is the family-friendly option: two mineral pools on the McKenzie River near McKenzie Bridge, open 365 days a year with lodging and camping. Swimsuits are required. The resort's website says walk-in visitors pay $12 per person per hour, one hour per day, between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m., and that full-day passes are no longer sold. Children must be at least 5. Overnight guests can soak from 9 a.m. to 9 p.m.

Is Umpqua Hot Springs Worth Visiting?

Yes: Umpqua Hot Springs is Oregon's most photographed wild soak, a set of small terraced pools on a mineral cliff above the North Umpqua River, and it is open year-round for day use. It is in the Umpqua National Forest in Douglas County, east of Roseburg near Toketee.

From Roseburg, take OR 138 east to milepost 59, turn left on Forest Road 34, cross the bridge and drive 2.2 miles, then turn right on Forest Road 3401 and go 0.7 mile to the parking area. A short, steep trail climbs from there to the pools. The trailhead page lists a $5 per vehicle day-use fee, waived with a Northwest Forest Pass or America the Beautiful pass on your dashboard. Since April 1, 2026, the trailhead is cashless: pay with the Recreation.gov app's Scan & Pay before you lose signal.

The Forest Service lists the area as day use only, sunrise to sunset, with no camping. The nearest campground is Toketee Lake. In winter, the road typically closes and visitors hike, snowshoe or ski the last 2.5 miles. In January 2026, a storm closed Forest Road 3401 until March 20, so check alerts before you go.

Are There Hot Springs Near Bend?

The only natural hot springs close to Bend are the shallow lakeshore pools at Paulina Lake, and they are reachable only in late spring and summer; for a year-round soak, Bend locals drive to Summer Lake or Terwilliger. If you are new to Bend or thinking of moving there, plan on a road trip for a real soak in winter.

Paulina Lake Hot Springs

Inside Newberry National Volcanic Monument, about 23 miles south of Bend on US 97 and then 14 miles east on Paulina-East Lake Road, a trail of a little over a mile runs north from Little Crater Campground to a handful of hand-dug pools in the sand at the lake's edge. They are primitive, shallow and best from about May through July; the road closes to cars in winter. Parking requires a Northwest Forest Pass, an America the Beautiful pass or the day-use fee.

Summer Lake Hot Springs

Summer Lake Hot Springs sits on OR 31 near Paisley in Lake County, with an old barn-style bathhouse, outdoor rock pools and wide views across the Summer Lake basin. The official website is clear: reservations are required and day use is not allowed, so book a cabin, house, RV site or tent site to soak. The indoor pool runs about 98°F and the outdoor rock pools about 104°F. The site posted that the indoor pool would close for the season after November 15, 2026, while the outdoor pools remain open.

What Are the Best Hot Springs in Eastern Oregon?

Crane Hot Springs near Burns, Alvord Hot Springs below Steens Mountain and Hot Lake Springs near La Grande are the three best-known soaks in eastern Oregon, and all three offer lodging or camping. Distances are long, so plan to stay the night.

Crane Hot Springs (Crystal Crane)

Many maps still call it Crystal Crane, but the owners now use Crane Hot Springs. It sits at 59315 OR 78 southeast of Burns in Harney County. The draw is a large, nearly seven-foot-deep hot pond that runs about 99 to 102°F, plus smaller private tubs, cabins, RV sites and camping. A March 2026 visitor guide reported hours of 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily, pond soaks of $5 to $15 for up to four hours and private tubs at $15 per person per hour. Confirm current rates.

Alvord Hot Springs

Alvord's twin concrete pools, which date to the 1940s, sit at the edge of the Alvord Desert playa with Steens Mountain rising behind. The official site lists 36095 E. Steens Road (mailing address Princeton, OR) and says the water leaves the ground at 170°F. Day-use soakers are welcome during business hours but should call ahead for availability; bunkhouses and campsites include soaking, and reservations are recommended.

Hot Lake Springs

Hot Lake Springs, at 66172 Highway 203 near La Grande, wraps a soak in history. According to Travel Oregon, the brick hotel was completed by 1908 with just over 100 rooms and later served as a sanitarium; restoration is ongoing. Day-use soaking is by reservation through the lodge, and the pub and movie theater are open to the public.

Are Oregon Hot Springs Open in Winter?

Most Oregon hot springs stay open in winter, but getting there is the hard part, because Forest Service roads to wild springs are not plowed.

  • Bagby: stays open while the road is passable. Once roads are impassable, the Forest Service stops maintaining the site until spring, usually April, and strongly discourages driving in on snow.
  • Terwilliger: open all year, but Forest Road 19 is not maintained for snow and ice.
  • Umpqua: the access road typically closes in winter, adding a 2.5-mile hike, snowshoe or ski each way.
  • Breitenbush: carry chains or use traction tires from November 1 to April 1.
  • Paulina Lake: the road into the monument closes to cars for the winter.
  • Resorts (Belknap, Crane, Summer Lake's outdoor pools, Hot Lake Springs) stay open year-round, but desert highways can ice over at night.

What Are the Rules, Fees and Etiquette?

Expect to pay $5 to $12 at wild springs, to carry cash or a phone app depending on the site, and to follow day-use hours that are enforced.

  • Passes: Umpqua accepts a Northwest Forest Pass or America the Beautiful pass. Bagby and Terwilliger do not accept passes; you pay the per-person fee.
  • Payment: Bagby is cash only at the trailhead. Umpqua is cashless through Recreation.gov.
  • Hours: Umpqua is sunrise to sunset. Terwilliger closes 10 p.m. to sunrise. Camping is not allowed at Bagby, Terwilliger or Umpqua springs themselves; use nearby campgrounds.
  • Clothing: Terwilliger and Breitenbush soaking areas are clothing optional, and clothing is optional at Bagby too, though many soakers wear swimsuits. Belknap requires swimsuits. Terwilliger prohibits visible nudity from the road.
  • Alcohol and pets: not allowed at Terwilliger or Breitenbush. Leave glass at home everywhere.
  • Etiquette: keep voices low, limit your soak when others are waiting, don't use soap in the pools, and pack out everything you bring.

How Do You Soak Safely at a Wild Hot Spring?

Test the water before you get in, keep your head above water and never soak alone after drinking.

  • Check the temperature. Source water at Bagby is 138°F and at Alvord 170°F. Mix or wait, and limit soaks in pools above about 104°F.
  • Keep your face out of natural pools and don't drink the water; untreated warm water can carry bacteria and amoebas.
  • Protect your car. The Forest Service warns against leaving valuables in vehicles at Bagby, and the same applies at any remote trailhead.
  • Plan for no signal. Download maps, tell someone your plan and carry warm layers and a headlamp.

FAQ

What is the best hot spring in Oregon?

For a wild soak, Umpqua Hot Springs and Terwilliger (Cougar) Hot Springs are the most popular. For a comfortable stay with lodging, Breitenbush, Summer Lake and Crane Hot Springs are favorites.

Are there free hot springs in Oregon?

Very few well-known springs are free. Bagby charges $5 per person, Terwilliger $12 per person for two hours and Umpqua $5 per vehicle (free with a Northwest Forest Pass or America the Beautiful pass).

What is the closest hot spring to Portland?

Bagby Hot Springs, about 40 miles southeast of Estacada in the Mt. Hood National Forest, is the closest wild spring to Portland. Breitenbush, near Detroit, is the closest retreat-style hot spring and requires a day-use reservation.

Do I need a reservation for Oregon hot springs?

Breitenbush requires day-use reservations, Summer Lake requires an overnight booking (no day use) and Hot Lake Springs takes day-soak reservations. Bagby, Terwilliger and Umpqua are first come, first served.

Are Oregon hot springs clothing optional?

Some are. Terwilliger and the soaking areas at Breitenbush are officially clothing optional, and clothing is optional at Bagby as well. Resorts such as Belknap require swimsuits.

Can you camp at Oregon hot springs?

Not at Bagby, Terwilliger or Umpqua themselves, which are day-use or no-camping areas; use nearby campgrounds such as Bagby Campground or Toketee Lake. Summer Lake, Crane, Alvord and Belknap all offer camping or RV sites.
