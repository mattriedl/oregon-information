About St. Paul

St. Paul is an incorporated city in Marion County, in Oregon’s Willamette Valley region, with a 2020 Census population of 434.

St. Paul or Saint Paul is a city in Marion County, Oregon, United States. It is named after the Saint Paul Mission founded by Archbishop François Norbert Blanchet, who arrived in the Oregon Country in 1838 to minister to the Catholic inhabitants of French Prairie. The population was 434 at the 2020 census. The city is part of the Salem Metropolitan Statistical Area.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the population of St. Paul, Oregon?

St. Paul had 434 residents at the 2020 U.S. Census (up from 421 in 2010).

Is St. Paul, Oregon affordable to live in?

St. Paul lies in the Willamette Valley region, where costs vary mainly by housing. Oregon charges no statewide sales tax.

What utilities do I set up when moving to St. Paul?

Electricity in this area is generally provided by Portland General Electric (confirm by address); natural gas, where available, by NW Natural, Avista or Cascade Natural Gas. Water, sewer, and trash are typically arranged through the City of St. Paul.

What is the job market like around St. Paul?

WorkSource Oregon offers free career coaching, and iMatchSkills is the state's largest job board.

Which schools serve families in St. Paul?

Assignment depends on your exact address. Start with the Oregon Department of Education directory or the Marion County district office.

How soon must I register my car after moving to St. Paul?

Within 30 days of establishing residency — both the vehicle registration and an Oregon driver's license.

Are there food banks or assistance programs near St. Paul?

Yes. Apply for SNAP at the ONE Oregon portal, find a pantry through Oregon Food Bank, or dial 211 for Marion County community resources.
