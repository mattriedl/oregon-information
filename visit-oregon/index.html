Best Time to Visit

Oregon has distinct seasons. Plan your trip around what you want to experience.

☀️

Summer

June – September

July through mid-September is Oregon's dry season, with almost no rain west of the Cascades and highs in the 70s–80s. It is peak time for Crater Lake (Rim Drive is usually fully open from early July), the Pacific Crest Trail and coast camping. Big events include the Oregon Shakespeare Festival in Ashland, the Oregon Country Fair near Eugene (July) and the Pendleton Round-Up (September). Book state park campsites and coast hotels months ahead — popular sites can fill up within days of opening.

🍂

Fall

October – November

September and October bring warm, clear days and thinner crowds — many locals call early fall the best time on the coast. Willamette Valley wineries celebrate harvest with tastings and events, and fall color peaks in mid-to-late October along the Columbia River Gorge and in Silver Falls State Park. Salmon return to rivers like the Sandy and the Rogue. Rain usually returns west of the Cascades by November, so pack layers.

❄️

Winter

December – February

Ski season typically runs from late November into spring at Mt. Hood Meadows, Timberline and Mt. Bachelor, and Sno-Parks require a winter parking permit. The coast is rainy but mild, and winter storms make for dramatic storm-watching in Cannon Beach, Depoe Bay and Bandon. Gray whales migrate south in late December — Oregon State Parks holds a Winter Whale Watch Week around the holidays. Check TripCheck before crossing mountain passes and carry chains.

🌸

Spring

March – May

March through May is peak waterfall season, as snowmelt fills Multnomah Falls and the Trail of Ten Falls at Silver Falls. The Wooden Shoe Tulip Fest near Woodburn (late March–April) and cherry blossoms on Portland's waterfront draw big crowds. Gray whales head north again in late March (Spring Whale Watch Week). Weather changes fast, and high-elevation roads — including Crater Lake's Rim Drive — usually stay closed by snow until June or July.

Oregon's Top Destinations

Eight places every first-time visitor should consider, from the coast to the high desert.

🌋 Crater Lake National Park

Oregon's only national park holds the deepest lake in the United States — 1,943 feet of startlingly blue water inside a collapsed volcano in Klamath County.

🪨 Cannon Beach & Haystack Rock

The 235-foot Haystack Rock rises from the sand in Clatsop County, with tide pools and nesting puffins at its base in spring and summer.

💧 Columbia River Gorge

An 80-mile National Scenic Area with 77 waterfalls on the Oregon side, including 620-foot Multnomah Falls, just 30 minutes east of Portland.

🧗 Smith Rock State Park

Known as the birthplace of American sport climbing, these golden cliffs above the Crooked River near Redmond also offer great hiking for non-climbers.

🌊 Silver Falls State Park

The Trail of Ten Falls is a 7.2-mile loop past 10 waterfalls — four of which you can walk behind — near Silverton in the Willamette Valley.

🏔️ Wallowa Lake & Mountains

Nicknamed "Oregon's Alps," the Wallowas tower over a glacial lake near the art-filled towns of Enterprise and Joseph in northeast Oregon.

🏜️ Oregon Dunes & Florence

The Oregon Dunes stretch about 40 miles along the central coast — the largest coastal sand dunes in North America — with dune buggy rides and hiking near Florence.

Oregon Road Trips

Three classic drives that show off Oregon's scenery.

🛣️ Highway 101 – The Oregon Coast

Drive all 363 miles of coastline from Astoria to Brookings, past lighthouses, sea stacks and towns like Cannon Beach and Newport. Allow 3–5 days to enjoy it.

Oregon Coast region →

🌲 Historic Columbia River Highway

About 30 miles of winding, historic road east of Portland lined with waterfalls, including Latourell, Wahkeena and Multnomah Falls, plus the Vista House viewpoint.

🌋 Crater Lake Rim Drive

A 33-mile loop around the caldera rim with more than 30 scenic overlooks. It is usually fully open only from early July to October because of heavy snow.

Oregon Outdoor Activities

Whatever you love to do outside, Oregon has a place for it.

  • 🥾 Hiking — The Pacific Crest Trail crosses the whole state, and Oregon has more than 250 state parks. Oregon State Parks →
  • ⛷️ Skiing — Mt. Hood offers skiing almost year-round on Timberline's Palmer Snowfield, and Mt. Bachelor near Bend is one of the largest ski areas in the West.
  • 🚣 Rafting — Run whitewater on the Deschutes, the Rogue (near Grants Pass) and the McKenzie River.
  • 🦭 Wildlife — Birdwatching at Malheur National Wildlife Refuge, Sea Lion Caves near Florence, and whale watching all along the coast.
  • 🚴 Cycling — Ride Willamette Valley wine-country routes, Bend's mountain bike trails, or the paved Banks-Vernonia State Trail.
  • 🍷 Wine — Over 700 Willamette Valley wineries, plus the Umpqua and Applegate valleys in Southern Oregon.

Visiting Oregon: Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need reservations for Oregon state parks?

Day use at most state parks does not need a reservation, though some charge a parking fee. Campsites, yurts and cabins should be reserved ahead — summer weekends on the coast often book up months in advance. Reservations open up to six months ahead through Oregon State Parks.

Is Oregon expensive to visit?

Oregon is moderately priced compared with California or Washington. There is no sales tax, so shopping and restaurant bills are lower than you might expect, but hotels charge lodging taxes. Coast hotels and Portland are the priciest in summer, while eastern and southern Oregon are much more affordable.

What is Oregon famous for?

Crater Lake, the Columbia River Gorge, a public coastline, Pinot Noir wine, Portland's food and coffee culture, craft beer, Nike, and outdoor adventure from the Cascades to the high desert.

Can you swim in Crater Lake?

Yes, but only at one spot: the bottom of the Cleetwood Cove Trail, a steep 2.2-mile round-trip hike that is normally open in summer only. The water is very cold — usually in the 50s°F at the surface even in August. Check the National Park Service website for current trail status.

What are the best beaches in Oregon?

Favorites include Cannon Beach (Haystack Rock), Oswald West State Park's Short Sand Beach, Pacific City (Cape Kiwanda), Newport's Nye Beach, the Oregon Dunes near Florence, and the sea stacks of Bandon. Every beach in Oregon is open to the public. See the Oregon Coast region guide.
